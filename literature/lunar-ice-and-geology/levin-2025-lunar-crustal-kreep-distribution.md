## Abstract (transcribed from title page)
The distribution of KREEP—potassium (K), rare earth elements (REE), and phosphorus (P)—in the lunar crust is an important clue to deciphering the geochemical and thermal evolution of the Moon. Surface measurements of thorium abundance taken by the Lunar Prospector Gamma Ray Spectrometer (LPGRS) instrument have shown that KREEP is concentrated on the lunar nearside surface, mirroring the hemispheric asymmetry observed in the distribution of maria, crustal thickness, and topography. However, the overall lateral and vertical distribution of KREEP within the crust is poorly constrained, leaving uncertainty in estimates of bulk crustal thorium abundance and in the history and evolution of KREEP. In this study, we compared the overall lateral and vertical distribution of lunar KREEP in the upper crust by determining the thorium abundance of material excavated by complex impact craters. We find that the distribution of KREEP on the nearside is consistent with a layer of high-Thorium ejecta from the Imbrium impact mixing with underlying low-Th (<1 ppm) crustal material, suggesting the excavation of a sub-crustal KREEP reservoir with thorium abundances as high as 45–120 ppm by the Imbrium-forming impact. Imbrium ejecta alone does not explain the distribution of thorium on the lunar farside, particularly around the South Pole Aitken basin, suggesting other sources for farside thorium enrichments. Furthermore, our results refute the existence of a large-scale Thorium-enriched layer in the upper 16 km of the farside crust.

## Summary

### Background / objective
KREEP is a geochemical component enriched in incompatible and heat-producing elements (K, REE, P, plus U and Th) formed from the final dregs of the lunar magma ocean. Its surface expression is concentrated in the nearside Procellarum KREEP Terrane (PKT), but its vertical and lateral distribution at depth is poorly constrained. Because LPGRS surface thorium measurements sample only the top ~20 cm, this study uses thorium signatures associated with complex impact craters (which excavate and redistribute subsurface material) to bound the thorium — and by extension KREEP — distribution within the upper crust and to test whether a buried Th-enriched layer exists.

### Methods / scope
Analyzes ~6,912 complex craters 20–200 km in diameter (Robbins 2019) using LPGRS thorium data (Lawrence et al. 2003) deconvolved against the instrument spatial response function (spacecraft altitude 30 km). For each crater, computes average [Th] of interior, ejecta, and background terrain, then fits a two-layer model (a Th-enriched top layer over low-Th anorthositic crust, [Th] = 0.63 ppm per Metzger et al. 1977). Two scenarios: Scenario 1 (Th-enriched top layer, e.g., Imbrium ejecta) and Scenario 2 (buried Th-enriched layer under anorthosite). Results grouped into 300-km-radius regions of interest (ROIs) on a 5°×5° grid to build global maps of layer thickness and minimum abundance. Ballistic sedimentation modeling used to relate Imbrium primary-ejecta thorium to the excavated sub-crustal reservoir.

### Key findings (with quantitative values)
All values below are text-extracted from the article body/tables; the underlying maps (Figures 1, 6, 7) are pseudocolor, but the reported medians/IQRs and reservoir figures are stated numerically in the text.

**Definitions / baselines:**
- KREEP conventionally defined as [Th] > 3.5 ppm; the PKT is outlined as the nearside region with Th > 3.5 ppm (Jolliff et al. 2000).
- Anorthositic (low-Th) crust taken as [Th] = 0.63 ppm.
- Assumed low-Th crustal thickness above the reservoir: 28 ± 7 km (Miljković et al. 2016; Taylor & Wieczorek 2014).

**Nearside vs. farside top-layer distribution (Scenario 1, hemispheric medians with IQR):**
- Maximum top-layer thickness: nearside median 2.0 km (IQR 1.7–2.4); farside median 1.6 km (IQR 1.3–2.0).
- Minimum thorium of top layer: nearside 2.9 ppm (IQR 2.3–5.0); farside 2.1 ppm (IQR 1.7–3.2).

**Imbrium ejecta and the sub-crustal KREEP reservoir (headline result):**
- Imbrium primary ejecta must have had [Th] between 25 and 35 ppm to explain the observed thorium in the discontinuous Imbrium ejecta blanket (1,200–3,300 km from Imbrium).
- Given ~28 km of overlying low-Th (0.63 ppm) crust, the excavated sub-crustal (KREEP reservoir) material must have had **[Th] = 45–120 ppm**.
- Abstract/Key Points state the reservoir has **at least ~26 ppm Th**.
- Volumetric calculation (Imbrium primary-ejecta paraboloid of diameter 721 km, excavation depth 72 km; 28 km of 0.63-ppm crust): the ~44 km of sub-crustal material excavated must contain a bulk [Th] of at least 33 ± 10 ppm (using a conservative 13-ppm primary-ejecta value); with the 25–35 ppm ejecta values this rises to 45–120 ppm.
- This exceeds the ~12 ppm commonly assumed for the nearside subsurface KREEP layer (Korotev 2000), implying greater nearside subsurface heating than previously assumed.
- KREEP reservoir thickness is not well constrained (model estimates 2.5–40 km); lunar mantle Th is much lower (~0.025 ppm).

**Buried-layer test (Scenario 2):**
- Nearside average minimum depth to a Th-enriched layer: 1.0 km (IQR 0.9–1.3); minimum [Th] of that layer 2.0 ppm (IQR 1.7–2.9).
- No large-scale buried Th-enriched layer detected in the upper 16 km of crust (maximum analysis depth ~16 km, set by the 200-km largest crater); results instead indicate vertical/lateral variability from impact mixing.
- Localized buried high-Th exceptions exist, e.g., Region A near Alphonsus crater (~13°S, 3°W): exceeds surface Th by ≥8 ppm at burial depth ≥3.5 km.

**Farside / South Pole–Aitken (SPA):**
- Imbrium ejecta alone does not explain farside thorium, especially around SPA. Elevated thorium in/around SPA exceeds values predicted by Imbrium-sourced KREEP by up to 2 ppm, consistent with SPA-exposed incompatible-element-enriched upper-mantle material (not a buried farside KREEP layer).
- Several non-PKT regions show subsurface Th exceeding surface by ≥1.0 ppm: Mare Australe, Compton-Belkovich, Hertzsprung, Korolev, Gagarin, Fitzgerald-Jackson, and SPA.

**Context comparisons (from text):**
- Apollo 14–17 mafic impact-melt breccias: 8–12 ppm Th (traditionally taken as PKT lower-crust proxy).
- Apollo 14 Fra Mauro impact-melt breccias: 17–25 ppm Th.
- Serenitatis impact may have sampled ~5 ppm Th KREEP material.
- Maximum observed LPGRS surface values ~13 ppm Th.

### Limitations
- The 45–120 ppm reservoir thorium is model-inferred (two-layer mixing + ballistic sedimentation), not a direct measurement; the lower bound depends on assumed pre-Imbrian crustal Th (0.63 ppm) and ejecta/target mixing ratios (if less mixing, primary ejecta could be as low as ~13 ppm).
- LPGRS spatial resolution (spatial response FWHM <80 km) limits detection of small high-grade features and vertical structure; analysis depth capped at ~16 km.
- Reservoir thickness unconstrained (2.5–40 km across prior models), so total KREEP mass/volume is not resolved.
- Farside enrichments (SPA) attributed to non-Imbrium sources are interpretive.

## Topic mapping (neutral)
- Where KREEP/thorium sits in the lunar crust: nearside PKT surface (Th > 3.5 ppm) vs. low-Th highland crust (0.63 ppm) vs. inferred sub-crustal reservoir (45–120 ppm Th, ≥~26 ppm) beneath ~28 km of crust.
- Nearside vs. SPA/farside thorium contrast (top-layer medians ~2.9 vs. ~2.1 ppm; SPA excess up to 2 ppm over Imbrium prediction) — relevant to resource-grade and heat-production estimates for REE/thorium.
- Depth/vertical distribution: no coherent buried Th layer in upper 16 km of farside; reservoir depth ~28 km below nearside surface, thickness 2.5–40 km (unconstrained). Supplements the flagged (non-load-bearing) thorium/REE paragraph.
