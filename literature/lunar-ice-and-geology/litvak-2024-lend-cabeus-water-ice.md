## Abstract

Analysis of collimated neutron spectrometer data from the LEND (Lunar Exploration Neutron Detector) instrument aboard NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter, using observations from 2009 to 2023, to estimate the subsurface water ice content of the permanently shadowed region Cabeus-1 inside the large Cabeus crater near the lunar south pole. The surface neutron albedo in and around Cabeus-1 correlates with relief height and average annual temperature. The average subsurface water ice content over the whole Cabeus-1 region is estimated at 0.49 plus or minus 0.05 percent by mass, with a maximum of about 0.7 percent at the crater bottom where the minimum average annual temperature occurs, coinciding with the LCROSS impact site.

---

### Background and objective

- Water ice is expected to be cold-trapped at the bottoms of permanently shadowed polar craters, where surface temperatures are only tens of degrees above absolute zero. Evidence has accumulated from Clementine bistatic radar, Lunar Prospector Neutron Spectrometer (LPNS) epithermal-neutron suppression, and later instruments.
- LPNS spatial resolution was about 45 km, larger than most PSRs, so it could not localize ice to individual craters. LEND, developed at IKI, uses a massive collimator to achieve a narrow field of view, enabling higher-resolution neutron mapping of specific PSRs.
- The objective is to quantify the subsurface water ice content specifically within Cabeus-1, the PSR that hosts the LCROSS impact site.

### Methods and scope

- The analysis uses LEND collimated epithermal-neutron observations accumulated from 2009 to 2023. Hydrogen (in water) moderates secondary neutrons produced by galactic-cosmic-ray bombardment, so a suppressed epithermal-neutron flux indicates hydrogen and hence water.
- The study relates the measured surface neutron albedo in and around Cabeus-1 to relief height and to the distribution of average annual surface temperatures, and converts the neutron suppression to a subsurface water-ice mass fraction.

### Key findings

- The average subsurface water ice content over the entire Cabeus-1 region is 0.49 plus or minus 0.05 percent by mass.
- The maximum value, about 0.7 percent by mass, occurs at the very bottom of the crater on the surface area with the minimum recorded average annual temperature.
- That maximum-ice site coincides with the LCROSS impact location, where the LCROSS experiment had confirmed a significant amount of near-surface water ice.
- Surface neutron albedo in and around Cabeus-1 correlates with both relief height and average annual temperature, consistent with colder, lower areas holding more ice.

### Limitations

- The reported ice contents are orbital neutron-spectrometer estimates averaged over the instrument's collimated footprint (a regional average and a within-crater maximum), not point measurements; they represent a different support volume than a local impact or sample measurement.
- The 0.49 percent area-average and 0.7 percent maximum are model-derived from neutron suppression and depend on the assumed regolith and burial parameters.
- The result is specific to Cabeus-1 and does not by itself establish ice content elsewhere; it constrains how far a high local value such as the LCROSS plume measurement generalizes across the crater.

### Topic mapping

- The introduction reviews the cold-trap hypothesis and the observational history (Clementine, LPNS, LRO instruments) and motivates LEND's collimated approach. The methods develop the neutron-moderation basis and the correlation of neutron albedo with relief and temperature. The results present the 0.49 plus or minus 0.05 percent Cabeus-1 average, the about 0.7 percent maximum at the coldest crater-bottom site, and the coincidence with the LCROSS impact location.
