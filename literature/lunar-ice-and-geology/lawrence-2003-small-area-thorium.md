## Abstract (transcribed from title page)
Using an improved understanding of the Lunar Prospector Gamma-Ray Spectrometer (LP-GRS) spatial footprint, the authors derive a new global thorium map with full-width, half-maximum spatial resolution of ~(80 km)², mapped on 0.5° × 0.5° pixels. The map enables identification and classification of **42 small-area (<[80 km]²) thorium features** across the lunar surface. Twenty of these, all in the nearside Procellarum KREEP Terrane (PKT), show a thorium-iron anticorrelation indicative of mixing between mare basalts and thorium-rich mafic impact-melt breccias (MIB); at least one farside location (Dewar crater) appears to have MIB-like abundances. The map also identifies high-thorium mare basalts (>3 µg/g) in southwestern Mare Tranquillitatis near the Apollo 11 site. With the better footprint understanding, the surface thorium abundance at the **Compton/Belkovich thorium anomaly is constrained to 40–55 µg/g** — higher than any other measured location on the lunar surface and higher than most samples. Using 1 km/pixel Clementine FeO plus footprint information, plausible thorium distributions around Kepler crater are obtained at 1 km/pixel, appearing as simple mixing of thorium-rich MIB compositions and high-thorium mare basalts.

## Summary

### Background / objective
Lunar Prospector GRS data had previously mapped thorium at 60 km × 60 km pixels (Lawrence et al. 1999, 2000), a scale too coarse to isolate features at or below the instrument footprint. This paper's objective is to (1) develop a modeled LP-GRS spatial response function, (2) use it to optimize spatial information without amplifying statistical noise, and (3) conduct a global survey identifying, classifying, and characterizing small-area thorium features. Thorium is the tracer for KREEP (K, REE, P — enriched in incompatible/heat-producing elements).

### Methods / scope
- New global thorium map built from smoothed low-altitude LP-GRS counting-rate data, tied to the absolute 150 km × 150 km abundances of Prettyman et al. (2002a, 2002b), on 0.5° × 0.5° (≈15 km) pixels; effective FWHM resolution ~(80 km)².
- Modeled spatial response function ("kappa function," width parameter s and tail parameter k) fit versus spacecraft altitude and latitude.
- "Small-area" defined as features on the order of (60–80 km)² or less; features searched for at a size near the full-width, full-maximum (FWFM, ~240 km, the ~1%-of-max point) footprint.
- Cross-comparison against a photographic airbrush map (Rosiek & Aeschliman 2001) and 1 km/pixel Clementine Spectral Reflectance FeO (Lucey et al. 2000).

### Key findings (with values)
- **42 small-area thorium features** identified and tabulated (Table 2), each named for the nearest crater/basin.
- **34 of 42 (81%) are high-thorium** relative to surroundings; **34 of 42 (81%)** correlate with an airbrush-map surface feature.
- FeO correlation: 14/42 (33%) show none; 8 (19%) positive (co-varying) correlation; **20 (48%) show thorium-iron anticorrelation**.
- Terrane distribution: **31 features (74%) nearside** — 25 in the PKT plus 6 on the PKT boundary; **11 features (26%) outside the PKT** — 9 in the Feldspathic Highlands Terrane (FHT), 1 in eastern mare, and **exactly 1 in the South Pole-Aitken (SPA) Terrane**.
- Five feature classes: Thorium-Iron Anticorrelation (**20 features, all in PKT**); Thorium-Iron Positive Correlation (**8 features**, only Herschel and Copernicus in PKT); plus thorium-only, morphology-only, and No-Correlation (**7 features**) classes.
- **Compton/Belkovich thorium anomaly (Table 2 #40; ~100°E, 61°N, between craters Compton [103.8°E, 55.3°N] and Belkovich [90.2°E, 61.1°N]):**
  - Constrained **surface thorium abundance = 40–55 µg/g**, "higher than any other LP-GRS measured thorium abundance on the lunar surface and higher than most sample abundance values."
  - Table 3 measured Th on 150×150 km² pixels = **7.2 ± 1.1 µg/g** (the smoothed/large-pixel value); the 40–55 µg/g is the deconvolved **surface estimate** at scale size ~40 km.
  - Feature size: measured FWHM S = **46.3 ± 4.4 km**; inferred feature scale size ranges 3–37.5 km; co-located Clementine bright albedo feature ~15 × 30 km. Had the source been much smaller than the albedo feature, surface Th could be **>100 µg/g** (deemed unreasonable and rejected).
  - Described as a compositionally unique location; most consistent with an evolved igneous (alkali anorthosite–type) lithology; an alkali-anorthosite rock there "could be as high as 40–55 µg/g" Th.
- **South Pole-Aitken small-area thorium:** The single SPA-Terrane small-area feature in Table 2 is **#1 Fizeau (−133.7°, −59°, high-Th)**. Separately, **Dewar crater (#20; 165.5°E, 2.7°S, 50 km dia., FHT, located NW of SPA Basin)** is a high-Th feature but with **relatively low abundance 2–2.5 µg/g**; it is highlighted as a rare farside location with abundances similar to thorium-rich MIBs. (Broad-scale intro context: **SPA basin interior ≈ 2–5 µg/g** thorium.)
- Global context values (introduction): Imbrium region **5–12 µg/g**; SPA basin **2–5 µg/g**; most highlands **<2 µg/g**; low-abundance histogram peak ≈ 0.4 µg/g (highlands).
- Kepler crater (#12; 38°W, 8.1°N, 31 km dia., PKT) is the strongest anticorrelation-class enhancement; thorium reaches up to ~15 µg/g in low-iron patches N and S of the crater; interpreted as simple mixing of MIB and high-Th mare basalt. Th-Fe AC class features generally span Th 3–11 µg/g, FeO 7–15 wt.%.
- Timocharis crater high-Th material inferred ~9 µg/g (matching Apennine Bench formation), smeared to a measured ~7 µg/g by the footprint.

### Limitations
- Feature selection biased toward high-abundance features (low-Th features have poorer counting statistics and are harder to identify); some features may have been missed.
- Absolute abundances are tied to the Prettyman et al. (2002) large-pixel calibration, not to Apollo/Luna ground truth (the authors argue against ground-truth calibration given footprint/sample size mismatch).
- Small-area surface abundances (e.g., Compton/Belkovich 40–55 µg/g) are model-dependent deconvolutions constrained by assumed feature size, not direct point measurements; low-Th features carry larger relative uncertainty.
- Compton/Belkovich thorium distribution shows an east/southeast asymmetry not tied to any albedo/morphological counterpart, so the mapped source may not be the only source.

## Topic mapping (neutral)
- Lunar Prospector GRS thorium mapping and small-area feature deconvolution — the instrument/data basis for reported PKT, FHT, and SPA thorium abundances.
- Compton/Belkovich anomaly (40–55 µg/g) as the highest-thorium, compositionally unique small-area "red spot" on the Moon — reference point for high-grade KREEP/thorium concentration.
- South Pole-Aitken thorium content (broad interior 2–5 µg/g; one small-area SPA-Terrane feature, Fizeau; Dewar 2–2.5 µg/g near SPA) — relevant to farside thorium/KREEP resource context.
- Thorium-iron anticorrelation as a tracer of mafic impact-melt breccia (MIB) / KREEP mixing.
