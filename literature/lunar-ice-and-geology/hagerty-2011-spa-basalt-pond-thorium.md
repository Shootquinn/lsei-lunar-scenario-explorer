## Abstract (transcribed from title page, paragraph [1])
Imbrian-aged basalt ponds, located on the floor of South Pole-Aitken (SPA) basin, are used to provide constraints on the composition and evolution of the far side lunar mantle. The authors use forward modeling of the Lunar Prospector Gamma Ray Spectrometer (LP-GRS) thorium data to suggest that at least five different and distinct portions of the far side lunar mantle contain little or no thorium as of the Imbrian Period. They also use spatial correlations between local thorium enhancements and nonmare material on top of the basalt ponds to support previous assertions that lower crustal materials exposed in SPA basin have elevated thorium abundances, consistent with noritic to gabbronoritic lithologies. They suggest the far-side lower crust experienced multiple intrusions of thorium-rich basaltic magmas prior to the formation of SPA basin. Because many of the ponds on the far side have elevated titanium abundances, they argue the far side experienced extensive fractional crystallization that likely produced a KREEP-like component. However, because the Imbrian-aged basalts contain no signs of elevated thorium, they propose the SPA impact event triggered transport of a KREEP-like component from the far side and concentrated it on the near side. Because of the correlation between basaltic ponds and basins within SPA, they suggest Imbrian-aged basaltic volcanism on the far side was driven by basin-induced decompressional melting.

## Summary

### Background / objective
SPA is one of the largest and oldest basins in the Solar System; hypervelocity impact models indicate it may have accessed the lower crust and/or upper mantle of the lunar far side. Thorium (Th) is an incompatible, energy-producing element concentrated in the hypothetical urKREEP layer (K, REE, P), is well-correlated with U, and is readily measured from orbit by LP-GRS — making it a proxy for urKREEP abundance/distribution. The objective is to use the ~52 distinct Imbrian-aged basalt ponds on the SPA floor as windows into the composition of the far-side mantle by determining their Th (and Ti) content, and to explain the origin of SPA's regional Th enhancement.

### Methods / scope
Forward modeling / spatially adaptive image restoration (Pixon deconvolution, per Lawrence et al. 2007) of LP-GRS Th data, whose native footprint is large (~80 km FWHM). For each geologic feature the authors assign a candidate Th abundance, forward-model the resulting Th map, and use chi-square analysis against the measured LP-GRS map to find the optimum Th value for the uncontaminated (uncovered) basalt exposures versus the nonmare cover. Th abundances of norites/gabbronorites in the lunar sample suite (their Table 2) are used for comparison. Scope: basalt ponds in Apollo, Bose, Ingenii, Leibnitz, Von Kármán and related SPA-floor basins/craters.

### Key findings (with quantitative values quoted from the page)
- **SPA basin regional average Th: 2–3 ppm** — enriched relative to the surrounding feldspathic highlands terrane; "The basin has an average Th content of 2–3 ppm" (Fig. 1 caption: "an average of 2–3 ppm Th").
- **Regional high approaching 6 ppm Th** in the northwestern portion of the basin ("a regional high approaching 6 ppm Th in the north-western portion"; also stated as SPA has "a regional Th high of approximately 6 ppm").
- **Uncovered basalt-pond exposures are consistent with <1 ppm Th** ("uncovered expanses of basalt ponds in SPA basin are consistent with having <1 ppm Th, indicating that the basalt ponds are not the source of the regional Th enhancement"). Modeled optima are effectively zero within error — best-fit occurs when uncontaminated basalt has 0.00 ppm Th; adding as little as 1.0 ppm breaks the fit.
- **Individual pond best-fit Th optima** (chi-square, no negative values allowed): **0.0 ± 0.9 ppm**, **0.00 ± 0.29 ppm**, **0.02 ± 0.14 ppm**, **0.00 ± 0.22 ppm**, **0.00 ± 0.14 ppm**, **0.08 ± 0.27 ppm** across the modeled ponds — i.e., essentially thorium-free basalt.
- **At least five distinct portions of the far-side mantle contain little or no Th** as of the Imbrian.
- **Local Th enhancements sit on nonmare (noritic/gabbronoritic) cover, not the basalt:** modeled highs of **~8 ppm Th** within a pond scene, and NE enhancements of **4–6 ppm Th** with **1–2 wt.% TiO₂**, consistent with noritic to gabbronoritic lithologies. Some contaminated pond areas show **1–3 ppm** (e.g., a southern portion at **2.7 ppm**, another at **3.0 ppm**).
- **Individual lithologic units can reach 8–10 ppm Th;** the model must explain "regional Th abundances of at least 6 ppm and local abundances of 8–10 ppm Th."
- **Norites/gabbronorites in the lunar sample suite have a Th range with mean 2–3 ppm Th**, similar to the average Th composition of the SPA basin floor.
- **SPA vs. near side:** the ~6 ppm regional value is **lower than Th values associated with the Procellarum KREEP Terrane (PKT)**; large regional enhancement (≥6 ppm) is obtainable only through large-scale magmatic fractionation.
- **Interpretation:** very low Th in the ponds means their mantle sources assimilated essentially no Th; Th-rich norites, Ti-rich basalts, and indigenous regional Th enhancements indicate the far-side mantle underwent extensive fractional crystallization (a KREEP-like component) that was later mobilized/concentrated on the near side by the SPA impact — leaving Ti-rich, Th-poor source regions for the Imbrian ponds.

### Limitations
- LP-GRS spatial resolution is coarse (~80 km FWHM); pond-scale Th is inferred through forward modeling/deconvolution rather than measured directly, so uncertainties are relatively large (e.g., ±0.9 ppm on near-zero values).
- Modeling forbids negative Th, so the "0.00" optima are one-sided; the robust statement is "<1 ppm," not a precise low value.
- Mechanism for how Th was incorporated into the noritic parent melts is left as "an open-ended question"; the KREEP-mobilization model relies substantially on prior work (Arkani-Hamed & Pentecost 2001).
- Table 2 (sample-suite Th) and figure numeric detail sit in tables/figures; some page-extracted table cells (headers "(wt.%) (wt.%) Th(ppm) Th(ppm)") did not linearize cleanly in text extraction.

## Topic mapping (neutral)
- SPA / far-side thorium abundances — headline datum: SPA floor averages **2–3 ppm Th**, regional high ~**6 ppm**, local nonmare units up to **8–10 ppm**, while the actual mare basalt ponds are **<1 ppm Th** (far-side mare are KREEP-poor).
- Heterogeneity of SPA Th: the Th signal is carried by noritic/gabbronoritic nonmare cover and a NW anomaly, not by the basalts — bearing on whether SPA is a KREEP-bearing vs KREEP-poor resource terrane.
- LP-GRS Th mapping + Pixon deconvolution methodology (links to the Lawrence/Wilson LP thorium lineage in this collection).
- Ti–Th decoupling on the far side (Ti-rich, Th-poor Imbrian basalts) and the urKREEP mobilization / near-side PKT concentration model.
- Comparison anchor: SPA regional Th (~6 ppm) is lower than the near-side Procellarum KREEP Terrane.
