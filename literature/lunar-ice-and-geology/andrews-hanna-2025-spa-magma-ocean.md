## Abstract (transcribed from title page)
The ancient South Pole–Aitken (SPA) impact basin formed during the earliest pre-Nectarian epoch, excavated more deeply than any other known basin, and lies on the less-explored far side. The tapering of the basin outline and the more gradual topographic and crustal-thickness transition toward the south support a **southward impact trajectory** (opposite the commonly assumed direction). A broad **thorium-rich and iron-rich ejecta deposit southwest of the basin** is consistent with partial excavation of late-stage magma-ocean liquids, indicating that thorium-rich magma-ocean liquids persisted only beneath the southwestern half of the basin at the time of impact — matching predictions for the transition from a global magma ocean to a local enrichment of K, REE and P (KREEP).

## Summary

### Background / objective
Uses SPA — the Moon's oldest and deepest confirmed impact basin — to test what the giant impact exposed of the deep interior, and to constrain the timing and composition of the residual lunar magma ocean that ultimately became KREEP. Directly on-topic: it quantifies the thorium content of the deep reservoir the impact tapped and links it to the KREEP terrane.

### Methods / scope
Combines GRAIL gravity + LOLA topography (basin shape/tapering, crustal-thickness gradients), Lunar Prospector GRS thorium and iron (FeO) surface abundances over SPA ejecta zones, impact-trajectory modeling, and a magma-ocean crystallization model (thickness/composition of residual melt vs. percent crystallized solid, PCS). Thorium used as the tracer because it is strongly partitioned into the residual melt.

### Key findings (with quantitative values)
**Surface thorium around SPA (Lunar Prospector GRS):**
- **Southwest ejecta: Th = 1.79 ± 0.03 ppm** (much greater than typical far-side highlands).
- **Far-side highland background: Th = 0.95 ± 0.02 ppm.**
- Area east of basin: Th = 1.17 ± 0.03 ppm.
- **Basin centre (melt sheet): Th = 2.78 ± 0.05 ppm** (highest local surface value).
- Accompanying FeO enrichment: ejecta ~0.28 ± 0.02 wt% vs highlands ~0.26 ± 0.02 wt% (small); the SW ejecta is both Th-rich and Ti-rich, requiring a deep late-stage-magma-ocean reservoir rather than plain highland crust.

**Inferred deep reservoir thorium (source region the impact excavated):**
- Th concentration in the residual magma ocean excavated by SPA: **1.8–12.8 ppm, preferred value ≈ 9 ppm** (depends on mixing ratio of excavated magma ocean vs crustal material). Excavation reached depths **up to ~80 km**.

**Magma-ocean crystallization state and the KREEP transition:**
- SPA impact modeled to occur at **98.8 percent crystallized solid (PCS)** (range 98.4–99.3%); a discontinuous residual magma ocean ~5 km thick then persisted **only beneath the SW half of the basin**.
- Ilmenite / Ti-spinel begin to crystallize at **>90 PCS**.
- Th in entrapped residual melt: **2.8–4.3 ppm at 98.8 PCS**, rising to **20–40 ppm at 99.9 PCS**.
- Final KREEP reservoir (PKT, from Imbrium-ejecta constraint): **Th = 13–75 ppm, preferred 28–36 ppm**; PKT crystallization was largely complete at **99.90 PCS**.
- **PKT established ≈ 4.34–4.37 Ga**; SPA basin age consistent at ~4.31–4.39 Ga.

### Interpretation
The SPA giant impact exposed/partially excavated the Moon's late-stage residual magma ocean — the incompatible-element-rich (Th, and by association K, REE, P) reservoir that elsewhere solidified into KREEP. This is the physical basis for expecting deep KREEP-affiliated, thorium-enriched material to be exposed at/near SPA, distinct from the near-side PKT.

## Limitations
- Deep-reservoir Th (9 ppm preferred) is model-inferred from surface ejecta + mixing assumptions, not a direct measurement; range spans 1.8–12.8 ppm.
- Surface Th/Fe from Lunar Prospector GRS at ~tens-of-km resolution; small high-grade outcrops unresolved.
- Southward-trajectory and PCS-timing conclusions depend on the crustal-thickness/magma-ocean model; alternative scenarios (e.g., 4.43 Ga KREEP solidification) discussed.

## Topic mapping (neutral)
