## Abstract (transcribed / lightly de-hyphenated from page 1)

The resource budget of Earth is limited. Rare-earth elements (REEs) are used across the world by society on a daily basis yet several of these elements have <2500 years of reserves left, based on current demand, mining operations, and technologies. With an increasing population, exploration of potential extraterrestrial REE resources is inevitable, with the Earth's Moon being a logical first target. Following lunar differentiation at ~4.50–4.45 Ga, a late-stage (after ~99% solidification) residual liquid enriched in Potassium (K), Rare-earth elements (REE), and Phosphorus (P), (or "KREEP") formed. Today, the KREEP-rich region underlies the Oceanus Procellarum and Imbrium Basin region on the lunar near-side (the Procellarum KREEP Terrain, PKT) and has been tentatively estimated at preserving 2.2 × 10^8 km^3 of KREEP-rich lithologies. The majority of lunar samples (Apollo, Luna, or meteoritic) contain REE-bearing minerals as trace phases, e.g., apatite and/or merrillite, with merrillite potentially contributing up to 3% of the PKT. Other lunar REE-bearing phases include monazite, yittrobetafite (up to 94,500 ppm yttrium), and tranquillityite (up to 4.6 wt% yttrium, up to 0.25 wt% neodymium); however, lunar sample REE abundances are low compared to terrestrial ores. At present, there is no geological, mineralogical, or chemical evidence to support REEs being present on the Moon in concentrations that would permit their classification as ores. However, the PKT region has not yet been mapped at high resolution, and certainly has the potential to yield higher REE concentrations at local scales (<10s of km). Beyond the Moon, Mars and other extraterrestrial materials are host to REEs in apatite, chevkinite-perrierite, merrillite, whitlockite, and xenotime; these phases are relatively minor components (<0.6% of total sample) yet dominate a sample's REE budget with abundances typically 1–2 orders of magnitude enriched relative to their host rock. No extraterrestrial REE ore has been identified yet; extraterrestrial materials are host to other resources (metals Fe, Al, Mg, PGEs; and water).

**Keywords:** rare-earth elements; Moon; extraterrestrial; space; Mars; meteorites.

## Summary

### Background / objective
Review of the potential for extraterrestrial rare-earth elements (REEs — the lanthanides plus Sc and Y) as feedstock, motivated by finite terrestrial reserves (USGS 2014: Earth REE reserves ~140 million tonnes; China ~55 Mt, USA ~13 Mt) and short reserve horizons (Eu <1000 yr; La, Ce, Pr, Nd, Ho, Y all <2500 yr at then-current demand). The Moon is treated as the logical first target, with Mars and meteorites covered secondarily.

### Methods / scope
Literature synthesis and compilation, not new sampling. Draws on Apollo/Luna returned samples, lunar and martian meteorites, primitive-mantle-normalized REE patterns, gamma-ray-spectrometer (Lunar Prospector; Mars Odyssey) Th/K maps, and the lunar magma ocean (LMO) / urKREEP differentiation model. Compares lunar and martian REE-bearing accessory phases against terrestrial ore context (Bayan Obo, China cited as the largest terrestrial REE accumulation).

### Key findings (with values)
- **KREEP reservoir scale:** Volume of KREEP-rich lithologies underlying Oceanus Procellarum / PKT tentatively estimated at **2.2 × 10^8 km^3**; total mass of KREEP rocks estimated at **~2.25 × 10^14 – 4.5 × 10^14 kg**, quoted as the "potential reserve" of REEs. Th abundances in the PKT reach **>12 ppm** in isolated locations (GRS map).
- **REE-hosting is trace-phase-dominated:** Most lunar samples carry REE-bearing minerals only as trace/minor phases (apatite Ca5(PO4)3(F,Cl); merrillite (Ca)(PO4)). Merrillite can constitute **up to 3% of a lithology's volume in the PKT** — still described as too small for economic extraction.
- **Apollo 14 "Big Bertha" yittrobetafite (sample 14321,1494)** — the highest-grade single REE phase quoted: **17.67 wt% Nb2O3; 4.33 wt% UO2 (38,200 ppm U); 2.38 wt% Nd2O3 (20,420 ppm Nd); 12.00 wt% Y2O3 (94,500 ppm Y); and 12.81 wt% total REEs.**
- **Tranquillityite** (lunar Y-Zr silicate): enriched in **Zr 12–13 wt%, Y 1.0–4.6 wt%, Nd 0.1–0.25 wt%.**
- **Apollo 14 Quartz Monzodiorite (whitlockite/merrillite-rich) fragments** — highest bulk REE of any reported lunar material (excluding merrillite separates). Impact-melt-breccia whole-rock ranges (Apollo 14/15/16/17): **La ~21.2–87.4 ppm; Ce ~55–224 ppm; Sm ~9.92–28.5 ppm; Lu ~0.92–3.82 ppm.** LREE-enriched (La/Lu 22.8–23.0; Ce/Sm 5.5–5.8; Ce/Lu 58.6–59.8). One 18.4-mg merrillite-rich particle (W-QMD 14161,7373) = 11% phosphates + 1% zircon, with La/Yb 4.78 (vs 3.38 in sample 15386).
- **Lunar monazite:** rare, small (0.5–3 µm to 10 µm × 3 µm inclusions); one Luna 24 regolith monazite showed a positive Eu anomaly and Th below detection.
- **Grade comparison / resource verdict:** Even Apollo 14/15 samples with REE up to **~4× urKREEP** remain **low relative to terrestrial ores**. The paper's explicit conclusion: "there is to date, no geological evidence to support mining the Moon for REEs yet." No lunar (or any extraterrestrial) material currently qualifies as an REE ore.
- **Beyond the Moon (Mars/meteorites):** REEs hosted mainly in merrillite (plus apatite, whitlockite, xenotime, chevkinite-perrierite); accessory phases <0.6% of sample but carry REE budgets 1–2 orders of magnitude above bulk rock. Martian NWA 7034 regolith breccia: La 13.7 ppm, Lu 4.3 ppm. Enriched basaltic shergottite NWA 7257: 4.4 ppm La, 8.4 ppm Nd, 4.7 ppm Gd, 0.43 ppm Lu. Depleted samples down to La 0.15 ppm. Martian merrillite REE abundances are significantly lower than lunar merrillite.
- **Terrestrial context values:** Ce is the 25th most abundant element on Earth at avg ~60 ppm; least-abundant REEs (Tm, Lu) <1 ppm. Largest terrestrial accumulation = Bayan Obo, China.
- **Co-located non-REE lunar resources:** water ice in polar regolith **5.6 ± 2.9 wt%** (LCROSS) → ~2900 Mt in upper 1 m; He-3; O2 (20 extraction approaches, 8 plausible); Al **10–18 wt% in highlands** anorthosite; Fe **~3 wt% in lunar crust**, ilmenite ~10% of regolith; Mg (MgO in olivine); basaltic glass; vacuum.

### Limitations (as stated by the authors)
- No lunar or extraterrestrial REE ore has been identified; abundances are low vs terrestrial ore grades — extraterrestrial materials are "not suitable REE-mining targets" at present.
- The PKT has **not yet been mapped at high resolution**; spatial resolution of Th/K maps is ~10s of km, so higher local-scale REE concentrations cannot be ruled out and remain unsampled (high-Th PKT interior never directly sampled).
- A pristine urKREEP signature has never been found; urKREEP composition is modelled, not measured.
- Mars: no sample-return; inferences rest on 107 martian meteorites (as of 1 Aug 2017) plus orbital/rover data; the complementary LREE-enriched martian reservoir remains unsampled.
- Merrillite volume (up to 3% PKT) is too small for economic extraction on current understanding.

## Topic mapping (neutral)

- **REE as extraterrestrial feedstock:** Core subject. Direct, sourced grade numbers for lunar and martian REE phases usable for feedstock-grade comparisons.
- **Lunar material REE abundances (KREEP / urKREEP / mare / highlands):** KREEP/PKT volume and mass estimates; trace-phase (apatite, merrillite, monazite, yittrobetafite, tranquillityite) grades in ppm and wt%; Eu-anomaly contrast between highlands (positive) and mare basalts (negative).
- **Comparison to terrestrial ore grades:** Qualitative and quantitative — lunar/martian REE abundances low vs terrestrial ores; Bayan Obo and USGS reserve figures as terrestrial baseline; "up to 4× urKREEP still sub-ore."
- **Resource assessment / economic verdict:** Explicit — no current ore-grade REE off-Earth; conclusion that mining the Moon for REEs is not yet justified, with caveat that high-resolution mapping/local deposits could change this.
- **Adjacent lunar resources (ISRU):** He-3, water ice (5.6 wt%), O2, Al, Mg, Fe, basaltic glass, vacuum — relevant to lunar economy / ISRU framing but not REE-specific.
- **Mars & meteorites:** Secondary; merrillite-dominated REE budgets, martian meteorite grades.
