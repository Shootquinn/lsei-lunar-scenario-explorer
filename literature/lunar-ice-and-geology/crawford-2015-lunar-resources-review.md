## Abstract (as transcribed / paraphrased from the paper)
Reviews the raw materials on the Moon that are of potential economic interest. Considers solar-wind volatiles (H, He including ³He, C, N), water ice at the poles, and elements concentrated by lunar geological processes (Fe, Ti, Al, Si, the rare earth elements, U and Th). Concludes that no single lunar resource is likely, on its own, to justify large-scale extraction for export to Earth, but that the Moon has abundant materials of value for supporting activity in cis-lunar space and a future space economy; a tiered utilization sequence (life-support volatiles and building materials first, then metals/semiconductors/nuclear feedstock) is proposed.

## Summary

### Background / objective
A comprehensive survey of lunar in-situ resources, framed against the alternative of asteroidal sources. Directly relevant to a materials/energy discussion because it quantifies REE, phosphorus (via KREEP), thorium and uranium abundances and assesses their utility as manufacturing feedstock and energy sources — the exact SPA/KREEP resource angle, not just polar water ice.

### Methods / scope
Literature synthesis of Apollo sample geochemistry (Papike, Haskin & Warren, Warren & Wasson), orbital remote sensing (Lunar Prospector GRS thorium maps; Kaguya GRS uranium map), and resource-economics reasoning. Element-by-element (volatiles, water, Fe, Ti, Al, Si, REE, Th, U, ³He).

### Key findings (with quantitative values)
**KREEP / REE / phosphorus.**
- KREEP = enrichment in **K, REE, P** (Warren & Wasson 1979); U and Th are also concentrated in KREEP-rich material, so their distribution can be mapped by orbital gamma-ray spectroscopy.
- KREEP-rich lithologies are mostly associated with **Oceanus Procellarum and the Imbrium Basin** on the NW near-side = the **Procellarum KREEP Terrane (PKT)** (Jolliff et al. 2000). KREEP concentrated in the final stages of magma-ocean crystallization ("urKREEP") and then exhumed by the Imbrium impact.
- **urKREEP total REE ≈ 1200 ppm (0.12 wt%)** (Warren & Wasson 1979). Individual variation: Y and Ce ≈ **300 ppm each**; Eu and Lu only **3–5 ppm each**.
- Some **Apollo 14 and 15 PKT samples** have REE abundances **2–4× the urKREEP value**. The highest-Th spots (max surficial Th) have **not yet been sampled directly**; higher-abundance small outcrops are below current remote-sensing resolution.
- For comparison, terrestrial economically-exploited REE ores contain **~0.1–10 wt% REE** (BGS 2011) — lunar REE are at/below the low end.

**Thorium and uranium (energy feedstock).**
- Lunar **U scales linearly with Th, U/Th ≈ 0.27** (Korotev 1998).
- Kaguya GRS first global lunar U map (Yamashita et al. 2010): **max surficial U ≈ 2 ppm** in the highest-Th areas of the PKT south of Imbrium; map resolution 130 km (higher local values likely unresolved).
- **urKREEP U ≈ 5 ppm** (Warren & Wasson 1979). Highly evolved lithologies (quartz monzodiorites, felsites) known with **U = 10–20 ppm**.
- Terrestrial low-grade U ore is **>100 ppm** (WNA 2012) — lunar U is well below current terrestrial ore grade.
- U (→ Pu, or Th → fissile ²³³U) proposed as feedstock for nuclear power/propulsion in space; applications not yet developed.

**Other feedstock metals (context).**
- Fe ~14–17 wt% in mare basalts (silicate-bound); native Fe in regolith ~0.3–0.5 wt%. Ti ~5–8 wt% in high-Ti mare basalts. Al ~10–18 wt% in highland regolith. Si ~20 wt% in lunar materials.
- Solar-wind H ≈ 46 ± 16 ppm in regolith; polar water ice ~5.6 ± 2.9 wt% at Cabeus (LCROSS, Colaprete 2010).

### Author's economic conclusion
No single lunar resource clearly justifies extraction for Earth export today (Earth has abundant REE and richer U ores). Value is in supporting cis-lunar activity; REE export "may have to wait until off-Earth markets are developed." Recommends in-situ investigation of high-Th/KREEP areas to test for exploitable REE/U/Th grades.

## Limitations
- Review, not new measurement; abundances inherited from cited primaries (Warren & Wasson 1979; Yamashita 2010; Prettyman 2006).
- Remote-sensing resolution (80–130 km) means the richest small outcrops are unresolved — quoted maxima are lower bounds on local grade.
- Preprint (author-submitted) version; pagination/wording may differ from the journal of record, and the journal DOI is not printed here.
