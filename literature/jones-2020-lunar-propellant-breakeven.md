## Abstract

Parametric cost model comparing two architectures for supplying hydrogen and oxygen propellant across a lunar campaign followed by a Mars campaign: a production architecture (launch vehicle plus lunar lander plus in-situ resource utilization system that makes propellant from lunar ice) versus a delivery architecture (launch vehicle plus lunar lander that ships the same propellant from Earth). Breakeven is defined as the point where the production architecture costs less than the delivery architecture. The deck sweeps propellant demand, campaign duration, and system performance and reliability, and reports which regimes favor each architecture.

---

### Background and objective

- Research question as stated: over what human campaigns to the Moon and Mars, and over what system capabilities, does lunar propellant production break even relative to delivery from Earth.
- The deck situates itself in a paper series. Paper 1 (SciTech 2019) asked how Earth-delivered versus Moon-delivered propellant compare on cost for human Mars missions, and reported that in that model the best lunar ISRU approach cost about $31 billion more, roughly twice as expensive, relative to Earth-based propellant. Paper 2 (IAC 2019) reported that lunar ISRU may provide significant cost savings at high propellant demands and long campaign durations, and that ISRU system reliability is key to the breakeven.

### Methods and scope

- Two lunar ISRU production approaches for H2/O2 propellant from lunar ice were parametrically modeled for mass and cost. The deck labels two ISRU models, "MREE" and "Duke," and sweeps them against an ISRU system lifetime axis.
- Lander and vehicle model inputs shown: a multi-functional lander with Isp 450 s and initial mass fraction 0.2 to 0.3; a 40 t to cislunar option at about $1 billion per unit and a 15 t to cislunar option at about $200 million per unit.
- The canonical case parameters: lunar campaign length 5 to 15 years; Mars campaign length 14 years; propellant demand of 17 t/year on the lunar surface and 59 t/year in cislunar space for Mars.
- Sensitivity axes swept: ISRU system lifetime at 1, 3, and 5 years; cislunar demand at 47, 59, and 71 t/year; lunar surface demand at 17 and 34 t/year; post-transition (Mars campaign) duration at 10, 14, and 20 years.

### Key findings

- The magnitude and duration of the lunar campaign, more than the magnitude and duration of the Mars campaign, drives the cost breakeven (p. 1 summary and Demand Sensitivity slide).
- Without high-performing, long-lifetime autonomous ISRU systems (stated as greater than 5 years before replacement), the cislunar propellant demand for a Mars campaign favors delivery from Earth.
- Lunar ISRU is most promising with highly reliable ISRU systems and efficient reusable landers supporting long lunar campaigns prior to the Mars campaign (Canonical Case results slide).
- For the Mars-mission sensitivity: without high ISRU lifetimes, longer Mars campaigns do not favor lunar ISRU; at sufficiently high lifetimes this trend reverses (Lunar ISRU for Mars Missions slide).
- Overall conclusion: the value of lunar propellant production to a human exploration campaign hinges on both technical performance and the nature of the campaign; a short lunar campaign followed by Mars exploration is unlikely to realize the cost savings from propellant production compared with a more ambitious lunar campaign.
