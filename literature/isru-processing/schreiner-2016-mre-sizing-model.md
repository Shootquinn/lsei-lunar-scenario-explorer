## Abstract

(Transcribed from the paper.) The authors present a parametric sizing model for a Molten Regolith Electrolysis (MRE) reactor that produces oxygen and molten metals from lunar regolith. The model is founded on regolith material-property models validated using data from Apollo samples and simulants. A multiphysics simulation of an MRE reactor is developed and used to generate a database linking reactor design and performance trends. A novel design methodology uses this database to parametrically design an MRE reactor that can (1) sustain the required current, operating temperature, and mass of molten regolith to meet a desired oxygen production level; (2) operate for long periods by protecting the reactor walls from corrosive molten regolith with a layer of solid "frozen" regolith; and (3) support a range of electrode separations for operational flexibility. Mass, power, and performance estimates are presented over a range of oxygen production levels, with sensitivity analyses for operating temperature, regolith feedstock composition, and degree of operational flexibility.

## Summary

### Background and objective
MRE (also called Molten Oxide Electrolysis) applies a voltage across two electrodes immersed in raw molten lunar regolith; oxygen gas is produced at the anode and molten iron, silicon, aluminum, and titanium at the cathode. It requires no consumable reagents (no hydrogen, methane, or flux) and can extract up to 95% of the oxygen from regolith; regolith is >40% oxygen by weight. Context motivating ISRU: launch cost quoted as ~$110,000/kg to the lunar surface (2015 dollars). This paper is the authoritative primary sizing model for MRE: given a target oxygen production rate, it outputs reactor mass, power, current, operating voltage, and geometry.

### Methods and scope
A multiphysics (electrochemical + thermal) simulation (from the companion paper Schreiner et al., 2015) is run in large parametric sweeps to build a database, which regression equations then compress into a fast sizing model. Reactor design must satisfy four criteria: required molten mass, required current, joule-heated cold-wall containment (a frozen-regolith layer insulating the walls, Hall-Heroult style), and an acceptable electrode-separation range. Current is set by Faraday's law adjusted for current efficiency. Three regolith types analyzed: Highlands, High-Ti Mare, Low-Ti Mare. Baseline runs use design margin 1.5 and batch times of 8-18 h. Results are explicitly for an UNOPTIMIZED reactor. Operating temperatures are reported in Kelvin (1850-2300 K); note 1850 K ~= 1577 C, 2000 K ~= 1727 C, 2300 K ~= 2027 C -- i.e., the "~1600 C" MRE regime corresponds to the ~1850 K low end.

### Key findings (emphasis: MRE specific energy, power, temperature, thermal-vs-electrical basis)
- **Total reactor power model (Eq. 19):** `P_total = Q_reg-heatup + P(dG) + Q_endo + Q_heat-loss`, i.e. four additive terms -- (1) sensible+latent heat to raise regolith from 298 K to operating temperature, (2) electrolysis reaction work from Gibbs free energy, (3) endothermic-reaction offset (enthalpy of formation minus Gibbs free energy), and (4) radiative heat loss to the environment. This is the explicit thermal-vs-electrical decomposition: reactor power is dominated by heat-up + heat-loss (thermal) plus the electrolysis electrical term. Heat-loss regression fit quality: R2 = 0.997, RMSE = 0.51 kW.
- **Worked power example (the load-bearing primary number):** for an oxygen production level of **500 kg O2/year**, the model predicts a required operating voltage of **~0.96 V while FeO is the primary species (current 1470 A)** and **~5.61 V while SiO2 is the primary species (current 250 A)**; both operating points give a **power consumption of 1.4 kW** (the design varies current inversely with voltage to hold power roughly constant / minimize peak power).
  - Napkin specific energy (DERIVED here from the paper's own 500 kg/yr <-> 1.4 kW pairing, NOT stated as a kWh/kg figure by the authors): 1.4 kW held continuously = 1.4 x 8760 h ~= 12,260 kWh/yr for 500 kg O2 => **~24.5 kWh per kg O2**. This assumes continuous year-round operation at the stated 1.4 kW; batch/duty-cycle effects would raise the instantaneous rate. Treat as order-of-magnitude and verify against the Fig. 10 / Fig. 13 power-vs-production curves (see limitation below).
- **Oxygen extraction yield (k_O2, kg O2 per kg regolith):** ~**0.15 at low operating temperature** to ~**0.375 at high operating temperature** (conclusion figures). Theoretical maxima: 0.454 Highlands, 0.417 High-Ti Mare, 0.423 Low-Ti Mare. Higher operating temperature electrolyzes more oxide species before core solidification, raising yield.
- **Current efficiency (fraction of Faraday-law oxygen actually produced):** Highlands 74% at 1850 K -> 82% above 2000 K (conclusion: 72.5% -> 82%); Mare 57% at 1850 K -> 72% at 2200 K (conclusion: 57.5% -> 70%). These are lower than the 90-100% often cited in the literature because those measurements use melts without FeO/TiO2/K2O/Na2O; realistic whole-batch raw-regolith efficiencies are lower. A conservative 50% current efficiency is used for MgO and 30% for FeO in places.
- **Operating temperature effect on size/power (Fig. 10):** higher operating temperature always reduces reactor MASS and sometimes reduces power. At low temperatures (1850-2150 K) reactor power rises with temperature (larger core-to-wall temperature drop => more heat loss); above ~2200 K the steep yield increase and higher electrical conductivity outweigh this.
- **Regolith-type effect (Fig. 13, circa 2000 K):** Highlands reactors are **33% less massive and require 60% less power** than Mare reactors at midrange operating temperature; at the low/high ends the difference shrinks. MRE is thus "reasonably feedstock insensitive" (Highlands covers 84% of the near side, 99% of the far side).
- **Single-reactor production ceiling vs design margin:** design margin 1.0 -> max ~5000 kg O2/yr; design margin 1.1 -> ~9500 kg O2/yr. Larger design margins widen the feasible electrode-separation range (operational flexibility) at the cost of more mass and power.
- **Co-product metals:** at high operating temperature the cumulative molten metal produced approaches the oxygen mass (example at 10,000 kg O2/yr / "10 mT O2/yr"). Metals alloy at the reactor bottom; post-reactor separation needed but should require little additional energy since the alloy is already molten.

### Limitations
- Results are for an UNOPTIMIZED reactor; the authors flag future optimization within a full ISRU system (power system, O2 liquefaction/storage).
- The paper does NOT state a single kWh/kg O2 specific-energy figure; the reader must derive it from the power (kW) vs production-rate (kg O2/yr) relationship. The mass/power-vs-production curves live in Figs. 10, 11, 13 (raster plots) whose numeric axis values are not machine-extractable from this PDF -- the 500 kg/yr <-> 1.4 kW worked example in the text is the cleanest transcribable anchor.
- Steady-state approximation: the multiphysics simulation is axis-symmetric and time-invariant; real MRE is time-varying as different oxides are sequentially reduced (voltage/endothermic sink change over the batch). Oxygen gas flow was omitted from the simulation for convergence.
- Current-efficiency and composition inputs carry real uncertainty; the model assumes oxides are reduced discretely in order of increasingly negative Gibbs free energy (in reality species co-reduce).
