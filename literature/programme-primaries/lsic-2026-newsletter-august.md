## Abstract

### Capability Area Updates

**Crosscutting Capabilities (CC).** June 18 telecon covered the lunar surface radiation environment: Dr. Yihua Zheng (NASA Goddard) on space-weather impacts and CCMC tools; Drs. Drew Turner and Zach Yokley (APL) on SELINE (Site-agnostic Energetic Lunar Ion and Neutron Environment), a lunar surface instrument planned for a future Artemis or CLPS launch no earlier than 2028; Dr. Justin Likar (APL) on radiation hardness assurance schema, including board-level single-event-effects testing.

The Dust, Regolith, and Surface Interface (DRSI) special-topic meetings continued in June and July: June 23 covered updates to NASA-STD-1008 (the "NASA Dust Test Standard"), presented by Dr. Josh Litofsky (NASA Johnson Space Center), covering edits made, edits still in development, and community feedback collected; July 28 featured Stephen Indyk (Honeybee Robotics) on Honeybee's landed payloads Lunar PlanetVac and Trident, which operated on the Moon in March 2025. The Aug. 20 telecon is announced on Dust Mitigation: Priorities and Planning, with NASA Johnson Space Center representatives Amy Fritz and Nathan Jimenez, Dr. Cecily Sunday (University of Toulouse, ISAE-SUPAERO) on dust-interaction modeling, and industry speakers from Moonprint Solutions (flexible covers) and Clear Solar Corporation (keeping solar panels clear of dust).

**Excavation and Construction (E&C).** The July 29 monthly meeting's theme was Lunar Regolith Construction Tools & Berm Design. Dr. Ryan Garvey (Outward Technologies) presented NASA SBIR/STTR work on plume-surface interactions and lunar launch/landing pads, using OpenFOAM (computational fluid dynamics) and LIGGGHTS-Public (discrete element modeling) to estimate lander damage from hypervelocity impacts.

Evan Bell (NASA Kennedy Space Center SwampWorks Lab) presented the Multifunction End Effector for Regolith Acquisition, Construction, and Transfer (MEERCAT) system and a newer iteration with compaction capability, the Planetary Automated Compaction Tool (PACT); MEERCAT/PACT is designed to attach to a robotic end-effector for site preparation and geotechnical characterization. The source reports a specific test result: in testing with two lunar simulants (ICN-LHT-1G and RDW-LHT-1G-h) under vacuum conditions at ambient temperature, MEERCAT/PACT achieved approximately 80-120% relative density. Community input was gathered toward a mapping of geotechnical engineering priorities to lunar infrastructure needs, to be advanced further at the September GENIE workshop; in August, APL E&C facilitators plan to present and refine a draft geotechnical infrastructure mapping matrix with the community.

**In Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU).** The July 15 monthly meeting's theme was recent results from NASA SBIR/STTR awards. Andrew Brewer (Outward Technologies) presented two regolith resource extraction technologies: one for continuous regolith feeding into an oxygen-extraction reactor, and one addressing the extraction process itself for both oxygen and metals; no quantitative performance figures for either are given in the source. Kevin Yu (Caltech) presented work on a hollow anode for molten regolith electrolysis, discussing feasibility and efficiency of the concept; no numeric efficiency value is given in the source.

The August 19 SPARC Workshop (11 AM-4 PM ET) is previewed with the same two objectives as in General Updates; U.S.-citizen registration was open through Aug. 18.

**Surface Power (SP).** June 25 extended telecon: Earl Isidro (NASA Johnson Space Center) presented NASA's Lunar Surface Power Exchange Connector (LSPEC) project, developing an electrical power connector for deployed lunar assets to exchange power at 120 V DC. Brian Elliott (TDA Research) presented low-temperature battery work: TDA has modified ALE 18650 cells to exceed 137 Wh/kg specific energy down to -60 degrees C, using low-temperature liquid electrolytes and a nanostructured polymer electrode coating, and is producing R&D cells for evaluation.

Brian Holler and Nicholas Uguccini (NASA Glenn Research Center) presented complementary low-temperature battery/electronics work: the ICE CHILL project (Integrated Cryo-operable Electronics to Condition Hibernation for Improved Li-ion Longevity) has shown through passive cell-hibernation testing that commercial Li-ion cells recover open-circuit voltage after reheating from cryogenic temperatures; the ICE PUCC testbed (Initial Cryogenic Evaluation of Principal Underlying Components and Circuits), built to support cryogenic electronics that must enable "cold start" operation at 50 K from solar power at lunar dawn, has found that cryogenic solutions exist for resistors, capacitors, MOSFETs, and diodes, with op-amps the present focus.

The July 23 telecon featured Jin Wang (Ohio State University) on Lunar Power Distribution Architectures, discussing modular circuits, a GaN-based multilevel converter topology, AC/DC architecture trade-offs, and bidirectional GaN devices. Upcoming: Aug. 27 networking telecon; Sept. 24 telecon with Geoffrey Landis (NASA Glenn Research Center) on power beaming for Moon Base.

### Feature: Lunar Surface Power Transmission Workshop recap

By the SP Capability Area team (Emmanuel Coleman, Jacob Gehrett, Will Yerkes, Sean Young). Recaps a virtual workshop held May 28, 2026, with 145 participants across LSIC, covering environmental effects on transmission infrastructure, terrestrial microgrid approaches, AC/DC/power-beaming architecture comparisons, and technology gaps in power electronics and protection schemes. Four panels: environmental considerations; lessons from terrestrial power microgrid architectures (Sandia National Laboratories microgrid modeling and planning tools; MIT Lincoln Laboratory/US Army Corps of Engineers tactical-microgrid control and communication standards, referred to as a possible model for a future lunar standard, with input from Tomas Elder of MITLL); lunar power architectures (AC and DC cable transmission, laser power transmission, with panelists from NASA Glenn, JPL, and Volta Space Technologies; Volta's LightPort power-beaming demonstration is noted as having already secured a CLPS payload ride); and power distribution/conditioning. Four stated overarching takeaways: (1) transmission technology choices depend on supply/demand scale, and schedule/funding/ConOps uncertainty complicates planning; (2) the lunar power grid will likely mix AC, DC, and laser power beaming, with stacked DC-DC conversion modules able to reach transmission-level voltages and module redundancy mitigating high-part-count reliability concerns; (3) cable deployment and connection ConOps remain undefined, including handling of differing floating reference grounds between landed assets, with LEO rendezvous/docking practice suggested as a possible analogy; (4) interface standards must balance interoperability with flexibility given incremental, organic grid growth. The recap states a full workshop report was recorded and is viewable on the LSIC website, but does not indicate a separate written technical report exists beyond the recording and this recap.

### Member Spotlight: Georgia Institute of Technology

By Brett Molina. Profiles Georgia Tech ahead of its role as October 2026 Fall Meeting host. Describes the Space Research Institute (SRI), launched July 2025 (executive director Jud Ready; VP for interdisciplinary research Julia Kubanek quoted on its convening role), the Center for Lunar Environment and Volatile Exploration Research (CLEVER, a NASA-funded SSERVI team involved in the PRIME1 and VIPER missions), the Institute for Matter and Systems' Marcus Nanotechnology Building cleanroom (described as the largest such facility in the southeastern US for biomedical/semiconductor device fabrication), and the Georgia Tech Space Mission Operations Center (GT MOC), used to operate university-built satellites. Profiles Shaspreet Kaur, a 2025 Georgia Tech mechanical engineering PhD, whose research focuses on extracting aluminum, silicon, and iron from lunar regolith; no process description or quantitative results for this work are given in the source. Lists Georgia Tech mission involvements: Orbital Calibration II (OrCa2, space domain awareness smallsat), NASA's Green Propulsion Dual Mode (GPDM) mission, multiple International Space Station Materials Experiment activities, and VISORS (Virtual Super-Resolution Optics Using Reconfigurable Swarms), a high-resolution solar-imaging swarm mission.

### Limitations

A newsletter/bulletin, not a technical report; content is predominantly announcement- and recap-level rather than peer-reviewed or data-rich, and is calibrated here accordingly (short relative to the 200-350-line target for research sources). The three items carrying an actual numeric result, MEERCAT/PACT relative density, TDA Research battery specific energy, and the ICE PUCC cryogenic component survey, are each reported at the level of detail the newsletter itself gives; no underlying test report, dataset, or methods paper is cited by name for any of the three, so figures cannot be checked against a primary source from this document alone. No figures, charts, or plots carrying quantitative content appear in the source; the images present are event/portrait photographs with captions, not data figures, so no figure read is flagged for this source.

**Named document/dataset pointer not otherwise pursued in this reference set.** The clearest pointer to a separate, citable document in this issue is NASA-STD-1008, the "NASA Dust Test Standard," discussed at the June 23 DRSI meeting as having new edits, additions, and previewed future changes (p. 4); this is a specific, numbered standard rather than a general announcement, and it is not held elsewhere in the project's reference set as of this summary. A second, weaker candidate is the Tactical Microgrid Standard developed by MIT Lincoln Laboratory with the US Army Corps of Engineers, named in the Feature Article as a possible model for a future lunar power-grid standard (p. 9); it is likewise not held in the reference set. Neither pointer is followed further here, consistent with this task's scope.
