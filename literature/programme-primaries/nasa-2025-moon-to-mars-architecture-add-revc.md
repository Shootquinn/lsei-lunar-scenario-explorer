## Abstract

The Moon to Mars Architecture Definition Document (ADD) is NASA's systems-engineering framework document for its Moon to Mars exploration campaign. It establishes the process of decomposing NASA's Moon to Mars Objectives into functions, use cases, and elements that can be allocated to implementable programs and projects, and it chronicles the annual evolution of the architecture. The document is explicitly not a manifest, requirements, or policy document; it is a technical document reflecting the approved program-of-record baseline, updated yearly through the Architecture Concept Review. Revision C reorganizes and streamlines the content and adds the Lunar Utility Rover and Lunar Nuclear Fission System elements and a new list of architecture-driven data gaps.

---

### Background and objective

- NASA's Moon to Mars Architecture provides a high-level, unifying structure (rules, guidelines, constraints) for the agency's crewed deep-space exploration, translating the long-term Moon to Mars Objectives into implementable systems and capabilities.
- The ADD's purpose is to communicate and chronicle the architecture for stakeholders: commercial industry, international partners, other US government organizations, and NASA implementing programs. It maintains traceability from objectives to responsible parties and iterates as solutions mature.
- It is a technical, not policy, document: it reflects changes to the program of record only after formal US government approval, and shows partnerships only once formalized through a contract or agreement.

### Methods and scope

- ESDMD established the architecture to decompose the Moon to Mars Objectives into crewed and robotic exploration systems. The document captures NASA programs, projects, and contributions enabling human exploration of the Moon, Mars, and beyond (it may not cover some independent robotic or non-NASA systems).
- Content structure: Section 1 (Architecture Foundations: purpose, scope, content structure); Section 2 (Architecture Components: the current state, including segments, sub-architectures, elements, and other exploration assets); Section 3 (Architecture Evolution: roadmapping, architecture-driven technology gaps, new element definition, and assessments); Appendix A (Rationale and Considerations); Appendix B (Architecture Decomposition: objective-to-function and use-case mappings, and element function mappings); Appendix C (Architecture Definition tasks, legacy decisions, and open tasks); Appendix D (Architecture-Driven Technology Gaps, cataloged and prioritized); Appendix E (Architecture-Driven Data Gaps); Appendix F (Terminology).
- The architecture is developed and updated iteratively through an annual Architecture Concept Review that solicits input across NASA mission directorates, centers, and technical authorities, and incorporates new technologies and partnerships.

### Key findings

- The architecture is organized into campaign segments: Human Lunar Return, Foundational Exploration, and Humans to Mars, with the objective decomposition and functional mappings tracing objectives down to use cases and functions for each.
- Revision B introduced four sub-architectures that cut across the campaign: Data Systems and Management, Infrastructure, In-situ Resource Utilization, and Robotics.
- The architecture elements with function mappings include Commercial Lunar Payload Services, Exploration EVA Systems, Exploration Ground Systems, Gateway, Human Landing System, Human-Class Delivery Lander, Initial Surface Habitat, Lunar Nuclear Fission System, Lunar Surface Cargo Lander, Lunar Terrain Vehicle, Lunar Utility Rover, Orion, Pressurized Rover, Space Communications and Navigation Networks, and Space Launch System.
- Revision C specifically adds the Lunar Utility Rover and the Lunar Nuclear Fission System elements, adds a new catalog of architecture-driven data gaps alongside the updated technology-gap catalog, and refines the architecture definition tasks (including three new results shaping future work).
- The document catalogs and prioritizes architecture-driven technology gaps (Appendix D) and architecture-driven data gaps (Appendix E), which capture the technology and information NASA needs to achieve its exploration objectives.

### Limitations

- The ADD is a framework and communication document, not a manifest, requirements specification, or policy; it does not commit missions, budgets, or schedules, and it reflects only the approved program of record at the time of publication.
- Coverage is scoped to NASA-enabled human exploration systems; some independent robotic or non-NASA systems supporting exploration goals may not be covered.
- The architecture is explicitly iterative and updated annually, so any specific element list, gap catalog, or decomposition is a snapshot as of Revision C (December 2025) rather than a fixed design.

### Topic mapping

- Section 1 (Architecture Foundations) states the purpose, scope, and content structure. Section 2 (Architecture Components) captures the segments (Human Lunar Return, Foundational Exploration, Humans to Mars), the four sub-architectures (Data Systems and Management, Infrastructure, In-situ Resource Utilization, Robotics), and the elements. Section 3 (Architecture Evolution) covers roadmapping, technology gaps, new element definition, and assessments. Appendix B provides the full objective decomposition and element function mappings (including the Lunar Nuclear Fission System and the ISRU-relevant utilization payloads). Appendices C, D, and E give the definition tasks, the prioritized technology-gap catalog, and the data-gap catalog. Appendix F provides acronyms, glossary, and quantity descriptors.
