## Abstract

Re-modeling of the 2009 LCROSS impact into Cabeus crater to constrain the pre-impact lunar sediment structure and the water ice concentration consistent with ground-based observations of the debris plume. Matching the observed plume lightcurve requires a layer of dirty ice whose ice concentration increases with depth, a pure regolith layer, and a high-tensile-strength layer about 6 meters below the surface that resists excavation. From the best-fit vertical albedo profiles, the pre-impact water ice mass in Cabeus is about 5 plus or minus 3.0 times 10^11 kg, and the water mass concentration is either 8.2 wt% (assuming water ice albedo 0.8 and regolith density 1.5 g/cm3) or 4.3 wt% (assuming regolith density 3.0 g/cm3), demonstrating that the derived concentration depends strongly on the assumed regolith density.

### Background and objective

- LCROSS (2009) impacted the spent Centaur upper stage into Cabeus crater near the south pole; a Shepherding Spacecraft took spectra of the ejecta plume, detecting water vapor and water ice grains. The original analysis gave a water mass concentration in the plume material of 5.6 plus or minus 2.9 wt% (Colaprete et al. 2010; Heldmann et al. 2015).
- Prior evidence for polar ice came from Lunar Prospector and LRO neutron-spectrometer hydrogen enhancements and from VIMS-type OH/H2O absorption bands. LCROSS confirmed the cold-trap hypothesis for Cabeus.
- The objective is to explore what pre-impact sediment structure and ice distribution are required to reproduce the ground-based plume observations, refining the water estimate.

### Methods and scope

- The authors model the debris plume lightcurve using layered pre-impact sediment: a dirty-ice layer with ice concentration increasing with depth, a pure regolith layer, and a subsurface layer near 6 m depth with high enough tensile strength to resist excavation (so it does not appear in the plume).
- Vertical albedo profiles from the best-fit model are used to back out the pre-impact ice mass and the water mass concentration, under stated assumptions for water ice albedo and regolith density.

### Key findings

- Pre-impact water ice mass within Cabeus crater is about 5 plus or minus 3.0 times 10^11 kg.
- Water mass concentration in the lunar sediment is 8.2 plus or minus 0.001 wt% assuming a water ice albedo of 0.8 and a regolith density of 1.5 g/cm3, or 4.3 plus or minus 0.01 wt% assuming a regolith density of 3.0 g/cm3. The concentration therefore ranges roughly 4.3 to 8.2 wt% depending on the assumed density.
- The model debris plume ice mass is about 108 kg, and the derived regolith and water masses in the plume are consistent with in-situ LCROSS measurements.
- A best fit requires ice concentration that increases with depth plus a competent (high tensile strength) layer near 6 m that was not excavated, indicating the ice is not uniformly distributed in the near-surface column.

### Limitations

- The derived water concentration is strongly model-dependent, spanning 4.3 to 8.2 wt% purely from the choice of assumed regolith density (3.0 versus 1.5 g/cm3); the stated formal uncertainties are much smaller than this modeling spread.
- The results are constrained by ground-based plume observations of a single impact and by the assumed layered structure and albedo values, not by direct sampling of the subsurface.
- The competent layer near 6 m is inferred from the plume behavior, and its exact composition is one of a few plausible options rather than a determined material.

### Topic mapping

- The introduction reviews the LCROSS mission, the original 5.6 plus or minus 2.9 wt% plume concentration, and the prior neutron and spectroscopic evidence for polar ice. The modeling sections develop the layered pre-impact sediment (dirty ice increasing with depth, pure regolith, competent layer near 6 m) and the lightcurve fit. The results give the pre-impact ice mass (about 5 times 10^11 kg), the density-dependent 8.2 versus 4.3 wt% concentrations, and the 108 kg model plume ice mass consistent with in-situ measurements.
