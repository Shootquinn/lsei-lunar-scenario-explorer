## Abstract

Program-level status review of NASA lunar in-situ resource utilization development across the six years since the start of the Artemis program. The deck lays out NASA's ISRU strategy (market transparency, technology and system development, mission insertion), the target commodities, the fleet of resource-exploration instruments and rovers, and the demonstrated technology-readiness status of oxygen and metal extraction from regolith and of polar ice and water mining. It reports which capabilities are ready for flight demonstration and which are still gated by missing resource knowledge.

---

### Background and objective

- NASA's stated ISRU goal is scalable production and utilization capability enabling industry to implement ISRU for Artemis, sustained human presence, and space commercialization. Three key objective areas are named: market transparency (defining customer needs for ISRU products), technology and system development (via solicitations, public-private partnerships, challenges, and internal and external investment), and mission insertion (resource exploration and flight demonstrations, and industry-led end-to-end production including a pilot plant).
- Target commodities listed: oxygen; water and hydrogen; bulk and refined regolith; raw and refined metals (Al, Fe, Ti); silicon and ceramics; construction feedstock; manufacturing feedstock; fuels, plastics, and hydrocarbons; and food and nutrient feedstock.

### Methods and scope

- The deck is a portfolio review rather than an experiment. It compiles resource-exploration missions and their instruments (a large matrix spanning landing-location, physical-properties, mineral-characterization, and volatile-measurement instruments across CLPS deliveries, VIPER, PRIME-1, Chandrayaan-3, SLIM, LUPEX, Luna-27, ProSPA, and others), and it summarizes the technology-readiness-level (TRL) progress of oxygen, metal, and water extraction processes.
- Historical anchor: the first focused lunar ISRU effort under Constellation (2006 to 2011) advanced oxygen-from-regolith options (hydrogen reduction as low risk / low performance, carbothermal reduction as medium, molten regolith electrolysis as high), with field campaigns at Mauna Kea (H2 reduction 2008, CH4 reduction 2010, polar water prospecting 2012).

### Key findings

- Carbothermal reduction is the highest-TRL oxygen-from-regolith technology, advanced to TRL 5. Reported performance: single melts equivalent to a production rate of 140 kg O2/yr; 5 consecutive melt operations under lunar vacuum and thermal conditions; oxygen extraction demonstrated at greater than 20 g O2 per kW-hr thermal; oxygen production yield greater than 20 percent gram O2 per gram regolith; and greater than 99.7 percent recovery of the carbon used in the melt. An integrated reactor and solar-concentrator system with Sierra Space was to be tested in 2025.
- Molten Regolith Electrolysis (MRE) has improved for oxygen and metal (Fe, Si, Al) extraction. Lunar Resources tested an MRE iron reactor under lunar conditions at KSC, processing 25 kg of highland simulant over 36 hours (24 hours of electrolysis) with measured oxygen production matching theoretical levels. Blue Origin is developing MRE and purification reactors with integrated lunar environmental testing planned for 2026.
- Hydrogen / carbon monoxide (H2/CO) reduction of mare regolith has advanced to TRL 5 (breadboards by Lockheed Martin and JSC tested in Hawaii in 2008; the MMOST system from Pioneer Astronautics, now Redwire). Vacuum vapor pyrolysis and a hydrochloric-reduction plus molten-salt-electrolysis route are advancing toward TRL 4.
- Water and ice mining: loose-regolith excavation reached TRL 5 (IPEx bucket-drum excavator demonstrated 10 metric tons in 5 days); hard icy-regolith excavation reached TRL 5 via the Break the Ice Challenge (teams excavating 12,000 kg, about 800 kg/day). Icy-regolith processing: the Lunar Auger Dryer ISRU (LADI) targeted 1.78 kg/hr water at 75 percent extraction efficiency but was cancelled before TRL 5 due to budget cuts; the Planetary Volatile Extractor (PVEx) coring reactor reached TRL 5/6 (a 5 cm inner diameter, 0.5 m long core at 4 to 6 wt% water gave 43 to 56 percent water extraction).
- Water capture and cleanup (Paragon Space with GRC and JSC) is at TRL 3/4 (cold-trap freeze distillation tested to 0.1 kg/hr water vapor flow). Water electrolysis: PEM is high TRL with clean water; Solid Oxide Electrolysis (OxEon) reached TRL 5 at mission-relevant scale (each stack up to 0.9 kg/hr O2 and 0.12 kg/hr H2 over thousands of hours); alkaline electrolysis of dirty water has unresolved corrosion issues.
- Top-level takeaways as stated: flight demonstrations can proceed with reasonable risk for oxygen and metal extraction from regolith, but lunar water and volatile extraction lacks sufficient resource knowledge to proceed without significant risk. There is no current NASA technology-development work on icy-regolith processing, and final designs await icy-regolith resource information not currently available.

### Limitations

- The document is a program-status slide deck. Numeric results are individual project outcomes reported as summary bullets, not a controlled dataset, and many are attributed to external companies (Sierra Space, Lunar Resources, Blue Origin, OxEon, Paragon) whose primary data are not in this source.
- The deck states its own principal caveat: water and volatile extraction is gated by missing resource knowledge, and no US instruments were then funded for icy-regolith sample acquisition and heating.
- TRL claims are point-in-time (May 2025) and some efforts noted are cancelled (LADI) or planned but not yet executed (2025 to 2026 integrated tests).

### Topic mapping

- The opening slides state the ISRU plan, objectives, and commodity list. The resource-exploration slides present the instrument-and-mission matrix and the argument for using existing instruments before investing in new ones. The Constellation slide gives the 2006 to 2011 historical TRL baseline. The Oxygen and Metals slides (Results to Date 1 and 2) carry the carbothermal, MRE, and H2/CO reduction TRL and performance numbers. The Ice and Water Mining slides (Results to Date 1 and 2) carry excavation, icy-regolith processing, water capture, and electrolysis status. The Main Takeaways slide states the flight-readiness split between oxygen/metal (ready) and water/volatile (knowledge-gated).
