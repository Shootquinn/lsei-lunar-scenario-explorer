# Convention on International Liability for Damage Caused by Space Objects (1972)

## Citation

United Nations General Assembly. (1971, November 29). *Convention on International Liability for
Damage Caused by Space Objects*, G.A. Res. 2777 (XXVI). Resolution adopted at the 1998th plenary
meeting of the twenty-sixth session; annexed Convention done in triplicate at London, Moscow, and
Washington, 29 March 1972. Reprinted at United Nations Office for Outer Space Affairs (UNOOSA), Our
Work / Space Law.

Publisher URL: none in source

## Abstract

Text of United Nations General Assembly resolution 2777 (XXVI), adopted 29 November 1971 at the
1998th plenary meeting, commending and annexing the Convention on International Liability for Damage
Caused by Space Objects. The annexed Convention, done in triplicate at London, Moscow, and Washington
on 29 March 1972, comprises 28 articles elaborating liability rules under the Treaty on Principles
Governing the Activities of States in the Exploration and Use of Outer Space. States absolute
liability for damage caused by a space object on the surface of the earth or to aircraft in flight
(Art. II); fault-based liability elsewhere (Art. III); joint and several liability and apportionment
among launching States (Arts. IV-V); exoneration grounds and their limits (Art. VI); a claims
procedure through diplomatic channels subject to a one-year limitation period and no requirement to
exhaust local remedies (Arts. VIII-XI); a compensation standard of international law and principles
of justice and equity aimed at restoring the pre-damage condition (Art. XII); and a three-member
Claims Commission mechanism for claims unresolved through diplomatic negotiation (Arts. XIV-XX). The
captured text states no entry-into-force date, ratification count, or list of signatory/States
Parties.

## Summary

### Background and objective

The document opens with the General Assembly resolution text, not the Convention itself. The
resolution's preamble reaffirms "the importance of international cooperation in the field of the
exploration and peaceful uses of outer space," and states a desire that liability rights and
obligations "as laid down in" the Treaty on Principles Governing the Activities of States in the
Exploration and Use of Outer Space (the Outer Space Treaty) be elaborated in a separate instrument.
It recalls seven prior GA resolutions by number and date concerning elaboration of a liability
agreement: 1963 (XVIII) of 13 December 1963, 2130 (XX) of 21 December 1965, 2222 (XXI) of 19
December 1966, 2345 (XXII) of 19 December 1967, 2453 B (XXIII) of 20 December 1968, 2601 B (XXIV) of
16 December 1969, and 2733 B (XXV) of 16 December 1970 — the last of which had urged the Committee on
the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space (COPUOS) to reach early agreement on a draft convention "embodying
the principles of a full measure of compensation to victims and effective procedures which would
lead to prompt and equitable settlement of claims." The resolution notes the Committee's report and
commends the work of its Legal Subcommittee.

The annexed Convention has its own preamble. It recognizes "the common interest of all mankind in
furthering the exploration and use of outer space for peaceful purposes," recalls the Outer Space
Treaty, and states the objective of elaborating "effective international rules and procedures
concerning liability for damage caused by space objects" so as to ensure prompt payment of "a full
and equitable measure of compensation to victims." The stated premise is that, notwithstanding
precautionary measures by launching States and international intergovernmental organizations,
damage from space objects "may on occasion be caused."

### Methods and scope

As a legal instrument rather than an empirical study, the source has no research methodology; its
"method" is the resolution/treaty drafting convention of a preambular recital of purpose followed by
operative provisions. The document as captured consists of: (1) the General Assembly resolution text
(four numbered operative paragraphs, a plenary-meeting citation, and a date); (2) the annexed
Convention, a preamble plus 28 numbered articles (Article I through Article XXVIII), several
subdivided into numbered and lettered paragraphs; and (3) a single numbered note citing "Official
Records of the General Assembly, Twenty-sixth Session, Supplement No. 20 (A/8420)" as the source of
the Committee report referenced in the resolution's preamble. The Convention's scope, per its own
terms, is liability of "launching States" for "damage" caused by "space objects," as defined in
Article I, and the procedural/institutional mechanisms — claims presentation, the Claims Commission —
through which liability is established and compensation obtained.

### Key findings

- **Resolution operative paragraphs (1-4):** commends the annexed Convention; requests the depositary
  governments to open it for signature and ratification "at the earliest possible date"; notes that a
  State may, on becoming a party, declare it will recognize as binding, vis-à-vis other States
  accepting the same obligation, the Claims Commission's decision in any dispute to which it becomes
  a party; and expresses hope for "the widest possible adherence to this Convention." Dated to the
  1998th plenary meeting, 29 November 1971.

- **Article I (definitions):** "damage" means loss of life, personal injury, or other impairment of
  health, or loss of or damage to property of States, persons (natural or juridical), or
  international intergovernmental organizations; "launching" includes attempted launching;
  "launching State" means a State that launches or procures the launching of a space object, or a
  State from whose territory or facility a space object is launched; "space object" includes
  component parts of a space object as well as its launch vehicle and parts thereof.

- **Article II (absolute liability):** a launching State "shall be absolutely liable to pay
  compensation for damage caused by its space object on the surface of the earth or to aircraft
  flight" — liability without a fault requirement for these two categories of damage.

- **Article III (fault-based liability elsewhere):** where a space object of one launching State
  causes damage elsewhere than on the surface of the earth to a space object of another launching
  State, or to persons or property aboard it, the causing State "shall be liable only if the damage
  is due to its fault or the fault of persons for whom it is responsible."

- **Articles IV-V (joint and several liability; apportionment):** Article IV covers damage to a third
  State from an incident between two launching States' space objects elsewhere than on the earth's
  surface: the first two States are jointly and severally liable to the third State, absolutely if
  its damage occurred on the surface or to aircraft in flight, on a fault basis otherwise; between
  themselves the compensation burden is apportioned by fault, or equally if fault cannot be
  established, without prejudice to the third State's right to seek full compensation from any or
  all jointly liable launching States. Article V makes States that jointly launch a space object
  jointly and severally liable for resulting damage; a paying State may seek indemnification from
  other joint-launch participants, who may agree among themselves on apportionment without prejudice
  to a damaged State's right to seek full compensation from any or all of them; and a State from
  whose territory or facility a space object is launched is itself a joint-launch participant.

- **Article VI (exoneration):** exoneration from absolute liability is granted to the extent a
  launching State establishes the damage resulted wholly or partially from gross negligence, or from
  an act or omission done with intent to cause damage, by a claimant State or persons it represents
  (para. 1). No exoneration is granted where the damage resulted from activities of a launching State
  "not in conformity with international law including, in particular," the UN Charter and the Outer
  Space Treaty (para. 2).

- **Article VII (exclusion for nationals/participants):** the Convention does not apply to damage
  caused by a launching State's space object to nationals of that launching State, or to foreign
  nationals while participating in that space object's operation from launching through descent, or
  while in the immediate vicinity of a planned launching or recovery area by that State's invitation.

- **Articles VIII-IX (presentation of claims):** a State suffering damage, or whose nationals suffer
  damage, may present a compensation claim to a launching State; if the State of nationality has not
  claimed, another State may claim for damage sustained in its territory; if neither has claimed or
  notified intent to claim, a State may claim for damage to its permanent residents (Art. VIII).
  Claims are presented "through diplomatic channels"; a State without diplomatic relations with the
  launching State may ask another State to present the claim, or present it through the UN
  Secretary-General provided both States are UN Members (Art. IX).

- **Article X (one-year limitation period):** a claim may be presented not later than one year
  following the damage's occurrence or identification of the liable launching State (para. 1); a
  State unaware of the damage or unable to identify the liable State may claim within one year of
  learning those facts, but never more than one year after it could reasonably have learned them
  through due diligence (para. 2); these limits apply even where the full extent of damage is
  unknown, but the claimant may revise and submit further documentation until one year after the
  full extent becomes known (para. 3).

- **Article XI (no exhaustion of local remedies):** presentation of a claim "shall not require the
  prior exhaustion of any local remedies" available to the claimant (para. 1); nothing bars pursuing
  a claim in a launching State's courts or tribunals instead, but a State may not present a Convention
  claim for damage already being pursued in such fora or under another binding international
  agreement (para. 2).

- **Article XII (compensation standard):** compensation "shall be determined in accordance with
  international law and the principles of justice and equity," to provide reparation that will
  "restore" the claimant (person, State, or international organization) "to the condition which
  would have existed if the damage had not occurred."

- **Article XIII (currency):** absent other agreement, compensation is paid in the claimant State's
  currency or, on its request, in the currency of the State from which compensation is due.

- **Articles XIV-XX (Claims Commission):** if diplomatic negotiations under Article IX yield no
  settlement within one year of the claimant's notification that it has submitted claim
  documentation, either party may request a Claims Commission (Art. XIV). The Commission has three
  members — one per party plus a jointly chosen Chairman, each appointment due within two months,
  with the UN Secretary-General appointing the Chairman within a further two months absent agreement
  within four (Art. XV). A party's failure to appoint lets the Chairman constitute a single-member
  Commission at the other party's request; vacancies follow the original appointment procedure; the
  Commission sets its own procedure, sitting place(s), and administrative matters; and, apart from
  single-member decisions, votes are by majority (Art. XVI). Multiple claimant or launching States
  joined in one proceeding do not enlarge the Commission — joined States collectively appoint one
  member each, failing which the Chairman constitutes a single-member Commission (Art. XVII). The
  Commission decides the claim's merits and any compensation payable (Art. XVIII), acting per Article
  XII's standard; its decision is final and binding if the parties so agreed, otherwise final and
  recommendatory (for the parties' good-faith consideration), with reasons stated; it decides no
  later than one year from its establishment absent a found-necessary extension; and it publishes its
  decision, delivering certified copies to the parties and the UN Secretary-General (Art. XIX).
  Expenses are borne equally by the parties unless the Commission decides otherwise (Art. XX).

- **Article XXI (large-scale danger):** where space-object damage presents a large-scale danger to
  human life, or seriously interferes with living conditions or the functioning of vital centres, the
  States Parties — particularly the launching State — are to examine rendering appropriate and rapid
  assistance to the damaged State on request, without affecting Parties' other rights or obligations.

- **Article XXII (intergovernmental organizations):** excepting Articles XXIV-XXVII, references to
  "States" extend to an intergovernmental organization conducting space activities if it declares
  acceptance of the Convention's obligations and a majority of its member States are Parties to both
  this Convention and the Outer Space Treaty (para. 1); member States that are Parties must ensure
  such a declaration is made (para. 2); an organization liable under the Convention, with its member
  States that are Parties, is jointly and severally liable, claims going first to the organization,
  with members' liability invokable only after six months' non-payment of an agreed or determined sum
  (para. 3); and a claim for damage to a declaring organization is presented by a member State that
  is a Party (para. 4).

- **Article XXIII (relation to other agreements):** the Convention does not affect other agreements
  in force between their parties (para. 1), and does not prevent States concluding agreements
  reaffirming, supplementing, or extending its provisions (para. 2).

- **Articles XXIV-XXVIII (final clauses):** open to all States for signature, with accession
  available after entry into force to non-signatories (Art. XXIV, para. 1); subject to ratification,
  instruments deposited with the UK, USSR, and US as Depositary Governments (para. 2); enters into
  force "on the deposit of the fifth instrument of ratification" (para. 3), and for later States on
  their own deposit date (para. 4); Depositary Governments notify signatory/acceding States of
  signature and deposit dates, entry into force, and other notices (para. 5); registered under UN
  Charter Article 102 (para. 6). Amendments may be proposed by any Party and enter into force for
  accepting Parties on acceptance by a Party majority, and thereafter for each remaining Party on its
  own acceptance (Art. XXV). Ten years after entry into force, review goes on the UN General
  Assembly's provisional agenda; a review conference may also be convened after five years in force,
  at one third of Parties' request with a majority's concurrence (Art. XXVI). A Party may withdraw
  one year after entry into force by written notice, effective one year after receipt (Art. XXVII).
  English, Russian, French, Spanish, and Chinese texts are equally authentic, deposited in the
  Depositary Governments' archives, with certified copies sent to signatory and acceding States (Art.
  XXVIII).

- **Closing clause and note:** the Convention closes "IN WITNESS WHEREOF the undersigned, duly
  authorized thereto, have signed this Convention," stating it was "DONE in triplicate, at the cities
  of London, Moscow and Washington, this twenty-ninth day of March, one thousand nine hundred and
  seventy-two" — 29 March 1972. A single footnote cites "Official Records of the General Assembly,
  Twenty-sixth Session, Supplement No. 20 (A/8420)" as the source of the Committee report mentioned
  in the resolution's preamble.

### Limitations

The source is a web-page text capture from the UN Office for Outer Space Affairs (UNOOSA) site,
undated as captured and without a recorded source URL; it retains page furniture (navigation labels
such as "Search," "About Us...," "Our Work...," "Space4SDGs...," "Events...," "Documents...," "COPUOS
2026..."; a "Links" block; the UNOOSA Vienna postal address; and a "Copyright ©2026 UNOOSA" footer)
that is not part of the treaty text and has been excluded from this summary. No entry-into-force date
appears in the captured text: Article XXIV states only that the Convention "shall enter into force on
the deposit of the fifth instrument of ratification," without stating when that deposit occurred or
naming any ratifying or acceding States. No list of signatory States or States Parties is present
anywhere in this capture — the document ends with the "DONE in triplicate" clause, the single
numbered note, and the word "Back," after which the captured text moves directly into site navigation
and footer material. The resolution's preambular reference to "the report of the Committee on the
Peaceful Uses of Outer Space" and the cited Official Records document (A/8420) are not themselves
included in this capture and could not be examined. As the Convention's own Article XXVIII notes,
English, Russian, French, Spanish, and Chinese texts are equally authentic; only the English text is
present here, so nuances of the other four authentic versions cannot be assessed from this source.
