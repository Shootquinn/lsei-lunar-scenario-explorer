# Agreement Governing the Activities of States on the Moon and Other Celestial Bodies (1979
Moon Agreement)

## Citation

United Nations General Assembly. (1979). *Agreement Governing the Activities of States on the
Moon and Other Celestial Bodies.* Resolution 34/68, adopted 5 December 1979 (89th plenary
meeting), annex. Official Records of the General Assembly, Thirty-fourth Session, Supplement
No. 20 (A/34/20), annex II. Opened for signature at United Nations Headquarters, New York, 18
December 1979.

Publisher URL: none in source

## Abstract

Text of UN General Assembly resolution 34/68 and its annexed 21-article treaty, the 1979 Moon
Agreement. Declares the moon and its natural resources the common heritage of mankind, bars
national appropriation of the moon by any means, and commits States Parties to establish an
international regime to govern resource exploitation once feasible. Sets peaceful-purposes and
demilitarization rules, freedom-of-scientific-investigation and sample-collection rights,
environmental-protection duties, reporting obligations to the UN Secretary-General, jurisdiction
and liability provisions, an inspection/consultation dispute mechanism, and final clauses on
signature, ratification, entry into force, amendment, review, and withdrawal.

## Summary

### Background and objective

The captured text opens with UN General Assembly resolution 34/68, adopted at the 89th plenary
meeting on 5 December 1979. The resolution's preamble reaffirms the importance of international
cooperation in the exploration and peaceful use of outer space, including the moon, and recalls
a sequence of prior General Assembly resolutions encouraging elaboration of a draft treaty on
the moon: resolution 2779 (XXVI) of 29 November 1971, and subsequently 2915 (XXVII) of 9
November 1972, 3182 (XXVIII) of 18 December 1973, 3234 (XXIX) of 12 November 1974, 3388 (XXX)
of 18 November 1975, 31/8 of 8 November 1976, 32/196 A of 20 December 1977, and 33/16 of 10
November 1978. It recalls in particular that resolution 33/16 endorsed a recommendation of the
Committee on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space (COPUOS) that its Legal Subcommittee continue, as
a priority, its work to complete the draft treaty. The resolution states that it was adopted
having considered the relevant part of the COPUOS report (paragraphs 62, 63, and 65) and noting
with satisfaction that COPUOS had completed the draft agreement text. Operatively, the General
Assembly commends the annexed Agreement, requests the Secretary-General to open it for
signature and ratification at the earliest possible date, and expresses its hope for the widest
possible adherence to it.

The annexed Agreement's own preamble states that the States Parties are noting the achievements
of States in exploring and using the moon and other celestial bodies; recognizing the moon's
role, as a natural satellite of the earth, in the exploration of outer space; determined to
promote, on the basis of equality, further cooperation among States in this field; desiring to
prevent the moon from becoming an area of international conflict; bearing in mind the benefits
that may derive from exploiting the moon's and other celestial bodies' natural resources;
recalling the Treaty on Principles Governing the Activities of States in the Exploration and
Use of Outer Space (the Outer Space Treaty), the Agreement on the Rescue of Astronauts, the
Return of Astronauts and the Return of Objects Launched into Outer Space (the Rescue
Agreement), the Convention on International Liability for Damage Caused by Space Objects, and
the Convention on Registration of Objects Launched into Outer Space; and taking into account
the need to define and develop the provisions of those instruments as they relate to the moon
and other celestial bodies. The stated closing act records that the undersigned, duly
authorized by their respective governments, signed the Agreement, "opened for signature at New
York on 18 December 1979."

### Methods and scope

As a General Assembly resolution and its annexed multilateral treaty text, the source has no
empirical method; its content is the operative and preambular text as adopted. Article 1
defines the Agreement's scope: its provisions on the moon apply also to other celestial bodies
within the solar system other than the earth, except insofar as specific legal norms enter
into force for a given body (para. 1); references to "the moon" include orbits around, or
other trajectories to or around, the moon (para. 2); and the Agreement does not apply to
extraterrestrial materials that reach the earth's surface by natural means (para. 3). Article
16 extends the Agreement's references to "States" — with the exception of the final-clause
articles 17 to 21 — to any international intergovernmental organization conducting space
activities, if the organization declares its acceptance of the Agreement's rights and
obligations and a majority of its member States are States Parties to both this Agreement and
the Outer Space Treaty.

### Key findings

- **Article 2** requires that all moon activities be carried out in accordance with
  international law, in particular the UN Charter and the 1970 Declaration on Principles of
  International Law concerning Friendly Relations and Co-operation among States, in the
  interest of peace and security, and with due regard to the interests of other States Parties.
- **Article 3** confines use of the moon to peaceful purposes and prohibits any threat or use of
  force, hostile act, or threat of hostile act on the moon or directed from it at the earth,
  the moon, spacecraft, spacecraft personnel, or man-made space objects (paras. 1–2); prohibits
  placing nuclear weapons or other weapons of mass destruction in orbit around, on a trajectory
  to or around, or on or in the moon (para. 3); and forbids military bases, installations,
  fortifications, weapons testing, and military manoeuvres on the moon, while not prohibiting
  military personnel for scientific or other peaceful purposes, or equipment and facilities
  necessary for peaceful exploration and use (para. 4).
- **Article 4** provides that the exploration and use of the moon "shall be the province of all
  mankind" and shall be carried out for the benefit and interests of all countries, irrespective
  of degree of economic or scientific development, with due regard paid to the interests of
  present and future generations and to promoting higher living standards and economic and
  social progress consistent with the UN Charter (para. 1); and that States Parties shall be
  guided by cooperation and mutual assistance, with international cooperation to be as wide as
  possible, whether multilateral, bilateral, or through international intergovernmental
  organizations (para. 2).
- **Article 5** requires States Parties to inform the Secretary-General, the public, and the
  international scientific community, to the greatest extent feasible and practicable, of
  moon-related activities — timing, purposes, locations, orbital parameters, and duration soon
  after each launch, and results on completion, with periodic reporting for missions over sixty
  days and only significant additions for missions over six months (para. 1); to promptly
  inform another State Party of the timing and plans of simultaneous operations in the same
  area, orbit, or trajectory (para. 2); and to promptly report any phenomena discovered in
  outer space, including the moon, that could endanger human life or health, and any indication
  of organic life (para. 3).
- **Article 6** establishes freedom of scientific investigation on the moon for all States
  Parties, without discrimination, on the basis of equality and in accordance with
  international law (para. 1); grants States Parties the right to collect on and remove from
  the moon samples of its mineral and other substances, which remain at the disposal of the
  collecting State Party for scientific use, with regard to be had to the desirability of
  making a portion available to other interested States Parties and the international
  scientific community, and further permits States Parties, in the course of scientific
  investigations, to use mineral and other substances of the moon "in quantities appropriate
  for the support of their missions" (para. 2); and records agreement on the desirability of
  exchanging scientific and other personnel on moon expeditions or installations to the
  greatest extent feasible and practicable (para. 3).
- **Article 7** requires measures to prevent disruption of the moon's existing environmental
  balance, whether through adverse change, harmful contamination, or otherwise, and measures to
  avoid harming the earth's environment through extraterrestrial matter or otherwise (para. 1);
  requires informing the Secretary-General of such measures and advance notice of radioactive
  material placements and their purposes (para. 2); and requires reporting to other States
  Parties and the Secretary-General on areas of special scientific interest for possible
  designation as international scientific preserves, with protective arrangements agreed in
  consultation with competent UN bodies (para. 3).
- **Articles 8–9** permit States Parties to pursue moon activities anywhere on or below its
  surface, including landing and launching space objects and placing personnel, vehicles,
  equipment, facilities, stations, and installations, which may move freely over or below the
  surface, without interfering with other States Parties' activities (Article 8); and allow
  States Parties to establish manned and unmanned stations, using only the area a station
  requires, with immediate and then annual notification to the Secretary-General of location,
  purposes, and continued use, and with stations installed so as not to impede other States
  Parties' free access to all areas of the moon (Article 9).
- **Article 10** requires States Parties to adopt all practicable measures to safeguard the
  life and health of persons on the moon, treating any person on the moon as an astronaut
  under Article V of the Outer Space Treaty and as spacecraft personnel under the Rescue
  Agreement (para. 1), and to offer shelter in their stations, installations, vehicles, and
  other facilities to persons in distress on the moon (para. 2).
- **Article 11** is the Agreement's central provision. It declares that the moon and its
  natural resources "are the common heritage of mankind," an expression finding its content in
  the Agreement's provisions, particularly paragraph 5 of the article (para. 1). The moon is
  "not subject to national appropriation by any claim of sovereignty, by means of use or
  occupation, or by any other means" (para. 2). Neither the surface nor the subsurface of the
  moon, nor any part of it or its natural resources in place, may become the property of any
  State, international intergovernmental or non-governmental organization, national
  organization or non-governmental entity, or natural person; placement of personnel, vehicles,
  equipment, facilities, stations, or installations, including structures connected to the
  surface or subsurface, creates no ownership right over the surface, the subsurface, or any
  area of the moon, without prejudice to the international regime described in paragraph 5
  (para. 3). States Parties have the right to explore and use the moon without discrimination,
  on the basis of equality and in accordance with international law and the Agreement's terms
  (para. 4). States Parties "undertake to establish an international regime, including
  appropriate procedures, to govern the exploitation of the natural resources of the moon as
  such exploitation is about to become feasible," to be implemented per Article 18 (para. 5).
  To facilitate that regime's establishment, States Parties must inform the Secretary-General,
  the public, and the international scientific community, to the greatest extent feasible and
  practicable, of any natural resources they discover on the moon (para. 6). The main purposes
  of the international regime to be established are stated as: (a) the orderly and safe
  development of the moon's natural resources; (b) the rational management of those resources;
  (c) the expansion of opportunities in the use of those resources; and (d) an equitable
  sharing by all States Parties of the benefits derived from those resources, giving special
  consideration to the interests and needs of developing countries and to the efforts of
  countries that have contributed, directly or indirectly, to exploration of the moon (para.
  7). All activities respecting the moon's natural resources must be carried out compatibly
  with the paragraph 7 purposes and with Article 6, paragraph 2 (para. 8).
- **Article 12** has States Parties retain jurisdiction and control over their personnel,
  vehicles, equipment, facilities, stations, and installations on the moon, with ownership
  unaffected by presence there (para. 1); routes vehicles, installations, equipment, or
  component parts found other than at their intended location to Article 5 of the Rescue
  Agreement (para. 2); and permits States Parties, in an emergency threatening human life, to
  use another State Party's equipment, vehicles, installations, facilities, or supplies on the
  moon, with prompt notification to the Secretary-General or the State Party concerned (para.
  3). **Article 13** requires a State Party that learns of a crash landing, forced landing, or
  other unintended landing of a space object or its component parts not launched by it to
  promptly inform the launching State Party and the Secretary-General.
- **Article 14** has States Parties bear international responsibility for national activities on
  the moon, whether carried on by governmental agencies or non-governmental entities, and for
  ensuring conformity with the Agreement, with non-governmental entities to engage in moon
  activities only under the authority and continuing supervision of the appropriate State Party
  (para. 1); and records that detailed liability arrangements, additional to the Outer Space
  Treaty and the Liability Convention, may become necessary as moon activities expand, to be
  elaborated via the Article 18 procedure (para. 2).
- **Article 15** lets each State Party assure itself that other States Parties' activities are
  compatible with the Agreement, with all space vehicles, equipment, facilities, stations, and
  installations on the moon open to other States Parties on reasonable advance notice of a
  projected visit, and inspection pursued alone, with another State Party's assistance, or
  through international procedures within the UN framework (para. 1). A State Party believing
  another is not fulfilling its obligations, or is interfering with its rights, may request
  consultations, which the receiving State must enter without delay, with other States Parties
  entitled to participate and the Secretary-General informed of the results (para. 2). If
  consultations do not yield a mutually acceptable settlement, the parties must pursue other
  peaceful means of their choice; any State Party may seek the Secretary-General's assistance
  without another's consent, and a State Party lacking diplomatic relations with another may
  participate through itself, another State Party, or the Secretary-General as intermediary
  (para. 3).
- **Articles 16–21** contain the final clauses. Article 16 extends the Agreement's references
  to "States" (excepting articles 17–21) to an international intergovernmental organization
  conducting space activities, conditioned on its declared acceptance of the Agreement and a
  majority of its member States being States Parties to both this Agreement and the Outer Space
  Treaty. Article 17 allows any State Party to propose amendments, entering into force for
  accepting States Parties on acceptance by a majority, and thereafter for each remaining State
  Party on its own acceptance. Article 18 requires a review question on the General Assembly's
  agenda ten years after entry into force, and lets the Secretary-General, as depositary,
  convene an earlier review conference (after five years in force) at the request of one third
  of the States Parties with a majority's concurrence, such a conference also to consider
  implementation of Article 11, paragraph 5. Article 19 opens the Agreement for signature by
  all States at UN Headquarters, New York, subject to ratification by signatories or accession
  by others, entering into force on the thirtieth day after deposit of the fifth instrument of
  ratification, and thereafter for each newly depositing State thirty days after its own
  deposit, with the Secretary-General to inform all signatory and acceding States of relevant
  dates. Article 20 allows a State Party to withdraw, by written notification to the
  Secretary-General, one year after entry into force, effective one year after receipt of the
  notification. Article 21 deposits the original Agreement — Arabic, Chinese, English, French,
  Russian, and Spanish texts equally authentic — with the Secretary-General, who sends
  certified copies to all signatory and acceding States. The closing formula records that the
  Agreement was "opened for signature at New York on 18 December 1979." A footnote to the
  resolution (numbered 1 in the source) cites Official Records of the General Assembly,
  Thirty-fourth Session, Supplement No. 20 (A/34/20), annex II, as the source text.

### Limitations

The captured text is a web-page rendering from the UN Office for Outer Space Affairs (UNOOSA)
site rather than a standalone document image. It carries substantial page furniture that is not
part of the treaty and has been excluded from this summary as content: site navigation labels
("Search," "About Us...," "Our Work...," "Space4SDGs...," "Documents...," "COPUOS 2026"), a
long "Our Work" and "Space Law" sidebar listing, a "Latest" news block with July and June 2026
items, a "Links" list, a Vienna contact address and phone/fax numbers, and a "Copyright ©2026
UNOOSA" footer. No URL was captured with the text, so provenance is limited to the file's own
content; the "2026" dates in the surrounding page furniture reflect the capture date, not the
Agreement. The source states the mechanism for entry into force (thirtieth day after the fifth
ratification, Article 19(3)) but does not itself state the calendar date on which that threshold
was reached, so no entry-into-force date is asserted here. Likewise, the text does not include a
list of signatory or ratifying States, so no adherence information can be reported beyond what
the articles themselves prescribe procedurally. The word "man uvres" in Article 3, paragraph 4,
appears as a broken rendering (apparently for "manoeuvres") and is reproduced here in corrected
form; this suggests minor transcription artifacts may be present elsewhere in the capture. The
footnoted Official Records report (A/34/20, paragraphs 62, 63, and 65) referenced in the
resolution's preamble is not itself included in this source.
