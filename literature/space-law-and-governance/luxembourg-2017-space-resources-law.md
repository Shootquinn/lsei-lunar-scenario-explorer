# Luxembourg Law of 20 July 2017 on the Exploration and Use of Space Resources

## Citation

Grand-Duché de Luxembourg. (2017). *Loi du 20 juillet 2017 sur l'exploration et l'utilisation des
ressources de l'espace* [Law of July 20th 2017 on the exploration and use of space resources].
Journal Officiel du Grand-Duché de Luxembourg, Mémorial A, No. 674, 28 July 2017. Unofficial English
translation; the French-language version is the authoritative text.

Publisher URL: none in source

## Abstract

National statute, 18 articles. Declares space resources capable of being owned and establishes a
mission-authorisation regime administered by the minister(s) responsible for the economy and space
activities. Sets eligibility conditions for operators (Luxembourg or European corporate form,
registered office in Luxembourg), governance, shareholder-suitability, financial-guarantee, and
audit requirements for authorisation; grounds for withdrawal; supervisory authority; operator
liability; and criminal penalties for unauthorised activity or breach of conditions.

## Summary

### Background and objective

The text is a four-page national statute enacted by the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg and published in
the Journal Officiel (Mémorial A, No. 674) on 28 July 2017. It creates a legal regime for the
exploration and commercial use of space resources, centered on a property-rights declaration
(Article 1) and an authorisation/licensing framework administered by designated ministers (Articles
2–18). The document supplied is an unofficial English translation; it states on its face that the
French version shall prevail.

### Methods and scope

As a statute rather than an empirical study, the document has no methodology in the research sense;
its "method" is legislative drafting and its "scope" is defined by its own articles. The law applies
to activities of exploring or using space resources for commercial purposes and to the operators
that carry them out. It expressly excludes satellite communications, orbital positions, and the use
of frequency bands from its scope (Article 2(4)). It does not itself define "space resources,"
"operator," or other terms beyond what is stated in the articles reproduced below; a Grand-Ducal
regulation is referenced (Article 13) as the vehicle for a subsequent fee-collection procedure,
indicating parts of the regime are left to implementing regulation.

### Key findings

- **Article 1** establishes the statute's central property-rights principle in a single sentence:
  "Space resources are capable of being owned."
- **Article 2** requires a written mission authorisation from "the minister or ministers in charge
  of the economy and space activities" before any person may explore or use space resources (para.
  1); bars pursuing the activity through another person or as an intermediary (para. 2); binds the
  authorised operator to the authorisation's conditions and to Luxembourg's international
  obligations (para. 3); and excludes satellite communications, orbital positions, and
  frequency-band use from the law's application (para. 4).
- **Article 3** provides that authorisation is granted to an operator for a mission of exploration
  and use of space resources for commercial purposes, upon written application to the ministers.
- **Article 4 (operator eligibility)** restricts authorisation to an applicant organised as a
  Luxembourg société anonyme, société en commandite par actions, or société à responsabilité
  limitée, or as a European Company (société européenne), in each case with its registered office in
  Luxembourg.
- **Article 5** makes the authorisation personal and non-assignable.
- **Article 6** requires the application to be accompanied by information useful for its assessment
  and by a mission program.
- **Article 7** conditions authorisation on evidence of a central administration and registered
  office in Luxembourg, including administrative and accounting structures (para. 1); a "robust
  scheme" of financial, technical, and statutory procedures for planning and implementing the
  mission (including commercialisation of resources), plus an internal governance scheme with
  defined organisational structure, risk-management processes, and internal controls (para. 2); with
  all such arrangements proportionate to the nature, scale, and complexity of the risks involved
  (para. 3).
- **Article 8** requires disclosure to the ministers of direct or indirect shareholders/members
  holding at least 10 percent of capital or voting rights (or, absent such a holder, the twenty
  largest shareholders/members), with authorisation refused if their suitability is unsatisfactory
  (para. 1). "Sound and prudent operation" is assessed against four criteria: (a) good repute of the
  operator and qualifying shareholders/members; (b) good repute, knowledge, skills, and experience
  of the management body of those shareholders/members; (c) their financial soundness; and (d)
  reasonable grounds to suspect money laundering or terrorist financing in connection with the
  mission (para. 2).
- **Article 9** requires the operator's management body members to be of good repute and to possess
  sufficient knowledge, skills, and experience at all times, assessed via police records and other
  evidence of irreproachable conduct (para. 1); requires at least two persons responsible for
  management, empowered to determine the direction of the business and possessing prior high-level
  professional experience in the space industry or a related sector (para. 2); requires advance
  notice to the ministers of any change in these persons, with the ministers empowered to request
  information and obliged to refuse a proposed change lacking adequate repute or experience, or
  posing a threat to sound and prudent management (para. 3); and requires the management body to
  proactively notify the ministers, in writing, of changes to the substantial information underlying
  the original authorisation (para. 4).
- **Article 10** requires the application to include a risk assessment of the mission, with risk
  coverage shown via personal financial means, an insurance policy from an undertaking outside the
  operator's group, or a guarantee from a credit institution outside the operator's group (para. 1);
  authorisation is conditional on financial bases appropriate to the mission's risks (para. 2).
- **Article 11** conditions authorisation on the operator's annual accounts being audited by one or
  more réviseurs d'entreprises agréés with adequate professional experience (para. 1); requires
  prior ministerial authorisation for any change of such auditors (para. 2); and applies the
  commissaire/supervisory-board rules of the Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies only
  where that law mandates them (para. 3).
- **Article 12** provides that the authorisation describes how the operator fulfils Articles 6
  through 11(1), and may additionally address: (a) activities carried on within or outside
  Luxembourg territory; (b) limits associated with the mission; (c) modalities for supervising the
  mission; and (d) conditions ensuring the operator's compliance with its obligations.
- **Article 13** sets a per-application fee, fixed by the ministers, ranging from 5,000 to 500,000
  euros depending on the application's complexity and workload, to cover administrative processing
  costs; a Grand-Ducal regulation is to set the fee-collection procedure.
- **Article 14** provides for withdrawal of authorisation if its granting conditions are no longer
  met (para. 1); if unused within thirty-six months of grant, renounced, or the business ceased for
  the preceding six months (para. 2); or if obtained through false statements or other irregular
  means (para. 3).
- **Article 15** places continuous supervision of authorised missions with the ministers.
- **Article 16** makes the authorised operator fully responsible for damage caused in connection
  with the mission, including preparatory works and duties.
- **Article 17** clarifies that a mission authorisation does not dispense with the need to obtain
  other approvals or authorisations.
- **Article 18** sets criminal penalties: contravening or attempting to contravene Article 2 carries
  imprisonment of eight days to five years and/or a fine of 5,000 to 1,250,000 euros (para. 1);
  contravening or attempting to contravene Article 5, Article 9(3) first subparagraph, Article 11(1)
  or (2), or the authorisation's terms carries imprisonment of eight days to one year and/or a fine
  of 1,250 to 500,000 euros (para. 2); and, without prejudice to paragraphs 1 and 2, a court may
  order discontinuance of a contravening operation under a penalty of up to 1,000,000 euros per day
  of infringement found (para. 3).

### Limitations

The source is an unofficial English translation of a French-language original; the document states
explicitly that the French version shall prevail, so specific terms (e.g., legal-form names, "good
repute," "sound and prudent operation") may carry precise meanings in French company and
financial-supervision law not fully recoverable from the translation alone. The statute itself
contains no definitions section, no preamble explaining legislative intent, and no explanatory
memorandum in this excerpt; background purpose is inferred from the operative articles only.
Implementing detail is deferred to a Grand-Ducal regulation for fee collection (Article 13) not
included in this document. The text does not name the specific ministries or ministers currently
holding "the economy and space activities" portfolio, only the functional designation. No case law,
administrative guidance, or record of authorisations granted under the law is included. As a
four-page, 18-article statute, the source offers no empirical data, methodology, or outcomes to
evaluate beyond its own legal text.
