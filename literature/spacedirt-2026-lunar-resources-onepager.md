## Abstract

A one-page, nine-row ranked list of lunar resource targets (solar energy/polar illumination; oxygen from dry regolith; bulk regolith for construction and shielding; polar water ice/volatiles; metals; helium-3; sites/cold traps; KREEP/rare earth elements; platinum-group metals), each scored on near-term value, readiness, certainty, and program demand, with per-row citations. The document states its ranks track the NASA/Artemis architecture as the primary demand signal and reflect the preparer's judgment, with adjacent ranks not materially distinguishable.

### Background and objective

Framed, like the companion capabilities one-pager, as context for space and non-space companies responding to a CSA RFI, prepared by SpaceDIRT Inc. It ranks resource targets rather than capability areas or companies.

Row-level narrative detail, as printed:

- Row 2: "Regolith is over 40 wt% oxygen [2] and supplies 75-80% of chemical propellant mass [4]. Carbothermal reduction is at TRL 5 [4]; Blue Alchemist passed CDR in 2025 [11]."
- Row 3: "Provides berms, landing pads, and radiation shielding. There is no practical substitute for regolith shielding on long-duration stays. Classified by USGS as a measured resource [1]."
- Row 4: "Would supply full propellant (H2 and O2) and life support if deposits prove out. The only ground-truth measurement (LCROSS) spans 0-11 wt% at 95% confidence [7], and no dataset resolves the 1-100 m scales a mine plan needs [5][6]."
- Row 5: "Recovered as co-products of oxygen extraction [8], with solar-cell-grade silicon already demonstrated [11]. Ilmenite also serves as the standard proxy for mapping helium-3 prospecting [2]."
- Row 6: "Average grade is about 4 ppb [2]; one kilogram requires processing at least 100,000 t of regolith [1]. The market is cryogenic refrigeration for quantum computing at roughly $20M/kg [11]. Interlune holds the only publicly announced signed lunar-resource purchase agreements [11]." As printed, this row states a market price of roughly $20M per kg and a processing requirement of at least 100,000 t of regolith per kg recovered, at an average grade of about 4 ppb; these are reported here exactly as printed, without conversion or reconciliation against any other figure held elsewhere in this project's reference set.
- Row 7: "Illuminated ridges within traverse of accessible cold traps are scarce and exhaustible; first movers gain a durable operational advantage, and legal tenure remains unsettled [10]. Strategic value, not extractive."
- Row 8: "urKREEP totals about 0.12 wt% rare earths, at or below the leanest grades mined on Earth, before transport [2]."
- Row 9: "IDA/STPI (2020) found lunar PGM export uneconomic before 2040 [9]; the serious PGM money targets asteroids, not the Moon."

### Limitations

**Extraction defect.** The task brief that accompanied this source's extraction identified character-interleaving in the companion capabilities document's row 4; independent review of this resources document's own extraction found the same defect present in row 1 (Solar energy / polar illumination), where the connecting narrative text is similarly scrambled into single-character and short-token runs. As with the capabilities document, the `_tables.txt` extraction was used as authoritative for the ranked-column cell values in row 1, which are reported above with confidence. The row's narrative prose (describing illumination duration, He-3 concentration in regolith, and USGS classification, based on legible fragments referencing "%," "the Moon," "average," "sunlight," "He-3," "power extraction," and "regolith below [2]") is not reliably reconstructable and is not reproduced or paraphrased here, to avoid presenting an assembled guess as a quotation.

Row 6's helium-3 figures (grade, processing ratio, market price) are stated in the source without a stated production-energy figure; this document does not itself make any claim about collection or processing energy per unit mass, so no such figure is reported for it here.

The document itself states its ranks reflect "the preparer's judgment" and that "adjacent ranks are not materially distinguishable."

### Topic mapping

Rows 1-3 map to power, oxygen, and bulk regolith as the near-term, better-characterized targets. Row 4 maps to polar water ice, framed as high potential but low current certainty. Rows 5-6 map to metals and helium-3 as co-product or niche targets. Rows 7-9 map to strategic site access, KREEP/rare earths, and platinum-group metals, the last three framed as low near-term value or off-Moon-focused.
