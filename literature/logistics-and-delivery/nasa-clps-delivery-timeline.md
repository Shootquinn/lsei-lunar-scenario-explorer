# NASA CLPS Delivery Timeline

## Citation

National Aeronautics and Space Administration. (n.d.). *Timeline of past and future CLPS deliveries* [Infographic].

Publisher URL: none in source

*Note: the graphic carries no printed publication or version date, so the year is given as `n.d.`*

## Abstract

Single-page NASA infographic. Plots eleven Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) task-order deliveries against a 2024-2028 horizontal year axis, each entry pairing a vendor with a named mission and a lander image. Four completed deliveries carry printed launch and landing dates; seven planned deliveries carry an asterisk and a year placement only. States no findings, methods, or analysis; it is a status-and-schedule reference at the moment of its (undated) production.

## Summary

### Background and objective

The graphic presents the delivery manifest of NASA's Commercial Lunar Payload Services initiative, under which the agency contracts commercial vendors to fly NASA payloads to the lunar surface. Its stated object is a single view of which CLPS deliveries have already flown and which remain scheduled, organized chronologically. It is a communications artifact rather than a study: the title is the whole of its thesis statement, and no narrative text, author, byline, or explanatory prose accompanies the timeline.

### Methods and scope

The layout is a horizontal timeline bar labeled with five year markers (2024, 2025, 2026, 2027, 2028). Missions are attached to the bar by short vertical leader lines and staggered above and below it; the above/below alternation is a spacing device and carries no evident meaning. Each entry consists of a circular lander image, a vendor name in bold, and a mission name beneath it. Completed deliveries additionally carry an italic block of launch and landing dates. Planned deliveries are marked with an asterisk on the vendor name.

Year bands are left-anchored: each year label sits at the left edge of the region belonging to that year, and band width scales with the number of entries in it (the 2025 band is roughly twice the width of the others and holds four entries). Scope is limited to CLPS surface deliveries. The graphic covers eleven deliveries across five vendors. It does not show payload manifests, landing sites, contract values, launch vehicles, or mission outcomes beyond the launch/landing facts printed on the four flown missions.

Further details recorded by the graphic:

- Four of the eleven deliveries had flown at the time of production; seven were planned.
- Of the four flown, three reached the surface. Peregrine Mission One is the sole entry recorded as not achieving a lunar landing, and it is the earliest entry on the timeline.
- Launch-to-landing intervals for the three successful landings, as computed from the printed dates: IM-1 seven days, IM-2 seven days, Blue Ghost Mission 1 forty-six days.
- Five vendors appear. Intuitive Machines carries four deliveries (IM-1 through IM-4), Firefly Aerospace three (Blue Ghost Missions 1, 2, and 3), Astrobotic Technology two (Peregrine Mission One, Griffin Mission One), and Blue Origin and Draper one each.
- Both flown Intuitive Machines missions and both flown Firefly/Astrobotic missions cluster in January and February of their respective years; the graphic offers no comment on this.
- Planned cadence declines across the horizon as drawn: four entries in 2025, two in 2026, two in 2027, one in 2028.
- Every entry is illustrated with a rendering or photograph of the corresponding lander.

### Limitations

- **Stated by the source.** A footnote states that future planned deliveries are placed on the timeline by year only, and that their order within a given year is not exact. Intra-year sequence therefore cannot be read from the graphic for any asterisked entry.
- **No date.** The graphic carries no printed publication, revision, or version date. Its currency cannot be established from the artifact itself, which matters for a schedule document whose planned entries are inherently perishable.
- **No provenance metadata.** No author, office, product number, or URL is printed. The only footer mark is a page number.
- **No sourcing or definitions.** The graphic does not say what qualifies an entry for inclusion, whether the manifest is exhaustive of awarded CLPS task orders, or where the dates originate.
- **Outcomes reduced to landing binary.** "No lunar landing" is the only failure language used, and no entry carries any statement of mission success, payload performance, or surface duration. Nothing distinguishes degrees of partial success.
- **Planned entries are unqualified.** No confidence, contract status, or schedule-risk indication accompanies any planned delivery; an asterisk is the only marker separating them from flown missions.
- **Year-band assignment is positional.** For the seven planned entries, which carry no printed dates, year membership must be inferred from horizontal placement against the year labels rather than read from text.
