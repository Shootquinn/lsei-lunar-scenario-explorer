# NASA CLPS Procurement Vignette

## Citation

National Aeronautics and Space Administration, Office of Procurement. (n.d.). *Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS)* [Vignette]. https://www.nasa.gov/content/commercial-lunar-payload-services-overview

Publisher URL: https://www.nasa.gov/content/commercial-lunar-payload-services-overview

## Abstract

Two-page NASA Office of Procurement fact sheet ("Vignette") on the Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) contract. Describes the contract vehicle and its administering center, the task-order competition process among a pool of contract holders, the stated contract value and period of performance, the awardee's end-to-end delivery responsibilities, the procurement's stated objectives, and the technology areas the program is meant to demonstrate. No empirical data, methodology, or findings in the research sense; content is programmatic/administrative description.

## Summary

### Background and objective

The document is a promotional/informational vignette issued by NASA's Office of Procurement describing the CLPS contract, framed under a Who/Where, What, How, Why structure. It situates CLPS within NASA's Artemis approach, positioning commercial lunar Moon flights carrying NASA-selected investigations and demonstrations as a means of studying the Moon. The Johnson Space Center (JSC) in Texas is identified as responsible for management of the CLPS Project Office and administration of the CLPS contract itself. No statement of authorship beyond "NASA Office of Procurement" and no publication date appear anywhere in the two pages.

### Methods and scope

The vignette is descriptive rather than analytical: it summarizes contract mechanics and policy objectives rather than reporting a study, experiment, or dataset. It covers (1) the contract's structure and administering authority, (2) the competitive task-order process, (3) contract value and duration, (4) the scope of awardee responsibilities under a task order, (5) five categories of stated procurement objectives, and (6) one illustrative example (a commercial lander). It closes with a single-sentence framing of CLPS's role in NASA's crewed lunar and Mars exploration goals, and a URL for further information.

### Key findings

- **Contract vehicle:** CLPS is described as a multiple-award, indefinite-delivery/indefinite-quantity (IDIQ) contract with a "combined maximum contract value of $2.6 billion" running "through November 2028."
- **Contract pool and competition:** Fourteen companies currently hold CLPS contracts, with contract terms allowing "periodic on-ramping of additional contractors." As payload requirements (surface and orbital) develop, each contract holder may compete for individual task orders, with selection based on "technical feasibility, price, and schedule." The contract is available for use by NASA centers and other government agencies, not JSC alone.
- **Administering office:** JSC manages the CLPS Project Office and administers the contract.
- **Awardee responsibilities (How):** Under an individual task order, the awardee holds end-to-end responsibility for commercial payload delivery — integrating, accommodating, transporting, and operating NASA payloads using contractor-provided assets, including launch vehicles, lunar lander spacecraft, lunar surface systems, Earth re-entry vehicles (where applicable), and associated resources. Companies are encouraged to fly commercial payloads alongside NASA payloads to stimulate private demand; NASA's stated long-term goal is to become "only one of many payload providers" seeking lunar or cis-lunar flight services.
- **Procurement objectives (Why), grouped under five headings:**
  - *Return Science*
    - Reduce costs and risk for "high priority planetary science."
    - Help ensure the strength of the lunar science community.
  - *Benefit Exploration*
    - Water, ice, and other volatile deposits framed as potentially key to future exploration architectures.
    - Characterize the nature of volatile deposits across lunar locations.
  - *Encourage Industry Participation*
    - Expand the economic sphere to include the Moon.
    - Foster initial R&D for lunar resource acquisition and processing.
    - Leverage existing investment in commercial lunar landers.
  - *Demonstrate Technologies*
    - Precision landing/hazard avoidance and ascent.
    - Improvements in power generation and storage.
    - Extreme-environment instruments/systems for lunar day/night survival and operations, described as relevant to technologies needed for future robotic missions to the icy moons of the outer solar system.
  - *Advance Planning for Mars and Other Destinations*
    - Enable deep-space architectures.
    - Reduce logistics chains from Earth.
    - Flight-qualify critical technologies such as precision landing and ascent vehicles.
- **Programmatic framing:** CLPS is described as "vital" to NASA's goal of sending "the first woman and first person of color to the Moon later this decade," to establishing sustainable lunar exploration, and to preparing for human exploration of Mars.
- **Illustrative example:** A concept image and caption describe the Astrobotic Peregrine robotic lander, stated to launch on a United Launch Alliance Vulcan Centaur rocket and deliver eleven NASA payloads to the Moon.

### Limitations

The document is an institutional procurement fact sheet, not a peer-reviewed or research publication; it presents programmatic claims and stated objectives without supporting data, methodology, or citations. No publication or revision date appears anywhere on either page, so its currency relative to the current CLPS contractor roster or contract ceiling cannot be established from the source itself — the fourteen-contractor count and the $2.6 billion/November 2028 figures should be read as a snapshot whose date is unknown. No authorship is attributed beyond the issuing office. The Astrobotic Peregrine example is illustrative rather than a report of mission outcome (no launch or landing result is stated).
