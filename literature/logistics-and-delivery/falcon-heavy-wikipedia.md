## Launch cost (LIST PRICE, commercial)

- Reusable configuration: **US$97 million** (2022 list)
- Fully expendable: **US$150 million** (2017 list)
- Historical: 2011 quoted $80-125M; 2013 quoted $77-135M
- Cost per kg, expendable: **~$2,350/kg to LEO**; ~$5,620/kg to GTO

These are the numbers the note's $100/kg import figure must be reconciled against. $100/kg sits ~23x below even the reusable-config effective rate and ~2,500x above the note's own 0.8 kWh/kg launch-energy physics. It is an aspirational Starship floor, not a demonstrated price.

## Development and reusability

- Private development, cost stated by Musk at **>US$500 million**, no government financing.
- Maiden flight 2026-02-06 (Tesla Roadster). Booster landings: 16 of 16 successful; center-core recovery succeeded once (Arabsat-6A, 2019).
- Certified for National Security Space Launch. Flew Psyche (2023) and Europa Clipper (2024).
- Expected to be superseded by Starship.

## Key specifications

- Height 70.0 m; total width 12.2 m; fueled mass 1,420,000 kg.
- 27 Merlin 1D engines on the first stage (9 per core); sea-level thrust 22.82 MN.
- Second stage: 1 Merlin 1D Vacuum, 934 kN. Propellant LOX / RP-1 kerosene.

## Comparison anchor

Delta IV Heavy (retired 2024): 28.4 t to LEO at ~$12,340/kg, versus Falcon Heavy ~$2,350/kg expendable. Establishes that a ~5x price collapse already occurred within one vehicle generation, which is the empirical basis for treating current list prices as a moving ceiling, not a floor.
