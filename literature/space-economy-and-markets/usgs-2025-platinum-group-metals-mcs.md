## Abstract

Two-page USGS data sheet on platinum-group metals (palladium, platinum, iridium, osmium, rhodium,
ruthenium). Tabulates 2020-2024e U.S. mine production, imports, exports, apparent consumption,
average annual prices, net import reliance, employment, world mine production and reserves by
country, world resources, government stockpile status, import sources, and a short prose section on
2024 events, trends, and substitution. A pure data/reference fact sheet; it reports market size and
price levels only and contains no elasticity or price-response modeling.

## Summary

### Background and objective

This entry is the platinum-group-metals (PGM) commodity page from the U.S. Geological Survey's
annual *Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025*, prepared by Ruth F. Schulte. The commodity group covers
six metals treated together for reporting: palladium, platinum, iridium, osmium, rhodium, and
ruthenium. Unless otherwise noted, quantity data are in kilograms of PGM content. The page follows
USGS's standard commodity-summary format: domestic production and use, a "Salient Statistics" table
for the United States (2020-2024e), recycling, import source shares, tariff and
depletion-allowance notes, an "Events, Trends, and Issues" section, a world mine-production-and-
reserves table by country, a world-resources note, and a substitutes note. As with all USGS
commodity summaries, the purpose is an annually updated, standardized statistical snapshot of U.S.
and world supply, consumption, trade, and price; it is not framed as an analytical or forecasting
document.

### Methods and scope

Data are compiled by USGS from company reporting, U.S. trade statistics, and outside price
reporting, not generated by USGS through its own market modeling. Mine production figures are
footnoted as "estimated from published sources." Import and export figures are drawn from Harmonized
Tariff Schedule and Schedule B trade codes and exclude waste-and-scrap trade from the
net-import-reliance calculation. Apparent consumption is defined as primary production plus
secondary production plus imports minus exports, excluding waste-and-scrap trade. Net import
reliance is defined as imports minus exports as a percentage of apparent consumption. Prices are the
"Engelhard unfabricated metal" average annual prices, sourced from S&P Global Platts Metals Week, a
private price-reporting service, not a USGS-derived price. World mine production and reserves are
reported by country for palladium and platinum specifically; the other four metals (iridium, osmium,
rhodium, ruthenium) appear in the U.S. import/export/price tables but are not broken out by
producing country in the world table. Reserve figures follow USGS Appendix C definitions; a note
states Russia's reserve figure was revised in this edition based on a government report and uses the
Russian A+B+C1+C2 classification system, where C2 deposits are those being developed or prepared for
development.

### Key findings

**U.S. mine production (kg PGM content):** Palladium fell from 10,300 (2023) to 8,000 (2024e);
platinum fell from 3,040 (2023) to 2,000 (2024e). One company, in Montana, mined and processed PGMs
domestically with an estimated value of about $310 million in 2024, down 42 percent from $541
million in 2023. Small additional PGM quantities were recovered as a byproduct of copper-nickel
mining in Michigan but were sold to foreign companies for refining rather than counted in domestic
processing. U.S. mine employment fell from 1,450 (2023) to 900 (2024e).

**World mine production (kg PGM content, by country, palladium and platinum only):** World total
(rounded) palladium production fell from 208,000 (2023) to 190,000 (2024e); world total platinum
production fell from 179,000 (2023) to 170,000 (2024e). By country for 2024e: Russia led palladium
production at 75,000, followed by South Africa at 72,000, Canada at 15,000, Zimbabwe at 15,000, the
United States at 8,000, and other countries at 4,200. South Africa led platinum production at
120,000, followed by Russia at 18,000, Zimbabwe at 19,000, Canada at 5,200, other countries at
4,600, and the United States at 2,000. The document does not print a single combined world
production total across all six PGMs; only palladium and platinum are broken out by country in the
world table.

**World reserves (kg PGM content, all six metals combined per country):** World total reserves
exceed 81,000,000 kg. By country: South Africa, 63,000,000; Russia, 16,000,000; Zimbabwe, 1,200,000;
United States, 820,000; Canada, 310,000; other countries not available. World resources (a broader
category than reserves) are estimated to total more than 100,000,000 kg, with the largest resources
and reserves located in the Bushveld Complex in South Africa.

**Prices (dollars per troy ounce, annual average, Engelhard unfabricated metal, S&P Global Platts
Metals Week):**
- Palladium: 2,205.27 (2020); 2,419.18 (2021); 2,133.81 (2022); 1,351.66 (2023); 980 (2024e).
- Platinum: 886.02 (2020); 1,094.31 (2021); 966.54 (2022); 973.00 (2023); 950 (2024e).
- Iridium: 1,633.51 (2020); 5,158.40 (2021); 4,581.93 (2022); 4,672.78 (2023); 4,800 (2024e).
- Rhodium: 11,205.06 (2020); 20,254.10 (2021); 15,585.00 (2022); 6,660.58 (2023); 4,600 (2024e).
- Ruthenium: 271.83 (2020); 576.12 (2021); 577.02 (2022); 466.49 (2023); 440 (2024e).
- No annual average price is printed for osmium; only trace import-for-consumption quantities (1 kg
  in most years, "-" i.e. zero in 2023) are reported for osmium.

Per the "Events, Trends, and Issues" note, estimated 2024 average prices versus 2023 changed as
follows: iridium up 3 percent; platinum down slightly; ruthenium down 6 percent; palladium down 27
percent; rhodium down 31 percent. The document attributes these 2024 price decreases to decreased
demand, investor uncertainty, and oversupply, stated as a qualitative attribution, not a quantified
elasticity or supply-response estimate.

**Net import reliance (percent of apparent consumption, United States):** Palladium: 34 (2020), 35
(2021), 31 (2022), 38 (2023), 36 (2024e). Platinum: 76 (2020), 75 (2021), 78 (2022), 83 (2023), 85
(2024e).

**Import sources (2020-2023 combined share):** Palladium: Russia 32 percent, South Africa 32
percent, Belgium 8 percent, Italy 8 percent, other 20 percent. Platinum: South Africa 45 percent,
Belgium 12 percent, Germany 10 percent, Italy 9 percent, other 24 percent.

**Recycling:** About 120,000 kg of palladium and platinum combined were recovered globally from new
and old scrap in 2024, including about 45,000 kg of palladium and 8,500 kg of platinum recovered
from automobile catalytic converters in the United States specifically.

**Domestic end uses and substitution:** The leading domestic use for PGMs is catalytic converters to
reduce harmful automobile emissions; other uses are catalysts for bulk-chemical production and
petroleum refining, dental and medical devices, electronic applications, glass manufacturing,
investment, jewelry, and laboratory equipment. Palladium has historically substituted for platinum
in most gasoline-engine catalytic converters owing to its historically lower price; about 25 percent
of palladium can routinely substitute for platinum in diesel catalytic converters, up to 50 percent
in some applications. For some industrial uses, one PGM can substitute for another, with efficiency
losses.

**Supply disruptions and trends (2024):** A domestic (Montana) mining operation was placed on
care-and-maintenance status with reduced employment, attributed to high operating costs and falling
PGM prices. South Africa, the world's leading producer of PGM-containing mined material, saw
production decrease owing to declining prices, higher deep-level mining costs, labor disputes, and
electricity-supply disruptions. Russia, the world's leading producer of mined palladium, saw
production decrease owing to natural-disaster disruptions, lower metal grades and ore recovery,
Russia-Ukraine conflict issues, and planned outages at a major metallurgical plant.

**Government stockpile and trade notes:** For both FY2024 and FY2025, potential disposals are listed
at 15 kg for iridium and 261 kg for platinum, with no potential acquisitions for either metal in
either fiscal year. All unwrought and semimanufactured PGM forms are imported duty free; the
percentage depletion allowance is 22 percent domestic and 14 percent foreign.

### Limitations

This is a compiled statistical fact sheet, not an analytical study: it reports production, trade,
price, reserve, and resource figures without modeling how a large new supply increment would affect
price. It supplies no price-elasticity estimate and should not be cited as a source for the
"flooding a market collapses the price" mechanism; it offers only the qualitative observation that
2024 oversupply coincided with falling prices for several of the six metals, alongside the market
size and current price levels a lunar or asteroid PGM-return revenue case would need to be checked
against. Several figures are marked estimated (2024 mine production and price figures, Russian
production) and are subject to later USGS revision. World production and reserve country breakdowns
are given only for palladium and platinum; the other four PGMs have no country-level breakdown, and
no combined six-metal world production total is printed. No annual average price is printed for
osmium. The specific per-commodity publisher URL was not printed on the two extracted pages; only
the general DOI supplied in the task instructions and the general MCS 2025 series URL are given
here, and the exact publisher landing-page URL should be independently confirmed before citation.
