## Abstract

Theoretical and observational study of lunar cold traps across spatial scales from 1 km down to about 1 cm, showing that many small unmapped "micro cold traps" substantially increase the total area where water ice can accumulate near the poles. Using a statistical topography model constrained by Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter shadow and temperature data, the paper estimates that micro cold traps hold roughly 10 to 20 percent of the Moon's permanent water cold-trap area, bringing the total cold-trap area to about 40,000 km2, with most of it poleward of 80 degrees latitude. The result implies polar water may be more accessible for future missions than large-crater estimates alone suggest.

### Background and objective

- Water ice is expected to be trapped in permanently shadowed regions (PSRs) at the lunar poles, where temperatures below about 110 K make the thermal lifetime of ice longer than the age of the solar system (the definition of a cold trap used here).
- Prior searches focused on large polar craters, where temperatures as low as 30 K have been measured, but the importance of small-scale shadows for cold-trapping had remained unclear. The paper sets out to quantify shadows and cold traps down to the smallest scale at which cold-trapping is effective for water ice.

### Methods and scope

- The authors build a statistical model of surface topography consistent with both the observed instantaneous shadow distribution and the observed temperature distribution from LRO.
- Theoretical models then connect instantaneous shadow to perennial (permanent) shadow and to temperature, allowing the fractional cold-trap area to be integrated over length scales from 1 km down to less than 1 cm (Equation 1 integrates the area occupied by cold traps between length scales as a function of latitude).
- The smallest scale at which cold-trapping is found to be effective for water ice is about 1 cm.

### Key findings

- Micro cold traps (small-scale shadows in the polar regions) contain approximately 10 to 20 percent of the Moon's permanent water cold-trap area and are the most numerous cold traps on the Moon.
- Including all spatial scales raises the total cold-trap area for water to about 40,000 km2, substantially more than previous large-crater estimates.
- A majority of water cold traps lie at latitudes greater than 80 degrees, because permanent shadows equatorward of 80 degrees are typically too warm to support ice accumulation.
- The distribution of many small cold traps implies lunar polar water may be more accessible as a resource than previously thought, since ice need not be confined to a few large deep craters.

### Limitations

- The results rest on a statistical topography model and thermal modeling constrained by orbital data, not on direct measurement of ice within micro cold traps; the presence of ice in any given micro cold trap is inferred from thermal stability, not observed.
- The paper notes that prior evidence for lunar ice deposits has been inconsistent despite a strong theoretical basis, so the cold-trap area is a capacity for ice accumulation rather than a measured ice inventory.
- Cold-trapping effectiveness is derived down to about 1 cm scale from models; behavior at the smallest scales depends on the assumed topography statistics.

### Topic mapping

- Section 1 (introduction) frames the PSR and cold-trap concept, the below-110 K threshold, and the prior focus on large craters. Section 2 (shadows) develops the statistical topography and shadow-area model (Equation 1). Subsequent sections connect instantaneous to perennial shadow and temperature, derive the 10 to 20 percent micro-cold-trap fraction and the 40,000 km2 total area, and discuss the latitude dependence (majority poleward of 80 degrees) and the accessibility implication for water as a resource.
