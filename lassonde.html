Lassonde Curve Explorer

The Lassonde curve is the valuation index of a lunar in-situ resource programme against years from 1 January 2030. The index rises through Exploration and Discovery, falls through Feasibility, rises from the trough through Construction and Rampup to 1.00 at the target time, and then falls through Reserve Depletion as the haul distance, the distance a rover drives from the extractor to the nearest unmined ground, grows (Equations 53 to 65). The template is the index shape before the target time, the standard curve is the same shape at target time 12.5 yr with a fixed yearly fall after it, and tT is the target time.

    On a focused handle, the arrow keys move 0.1 yr or 0.01 height. Shift multiplies the move by ten. Home and End go to the range limits. The evaluation marker is the dot labelled t, and a pointer drag on it moves time and height together. It has two Tab stops: the first moves time and the second is the height slider, where PageUp and PageDown also move 0.01. Escape returns the handle to its default, and on either stop of the evaluation marker it returns time and height together.

    The solid curve after tT, called the tail in the readouts below, falls as the haul distance grows and passes through the dot; IdxModel reads Equation 64 on the programme value for comparison. The curve is for a typical single site with one main in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) facility whose annual capability grows while haul distance grows; opening additional sites is outside the curve. Haul distance grows with the cumulative excavated area. The round trip lengthens, and each rover delivers fewer tonnes per shift. The site's capability grows with the plant and rover mass landing each year, and that growth offsets most of the longer haul. At the preset, the remainder is the decay rate d, the fall in the index in the first year after tT. The curve peaks at 1.00 at the target time and turns into the tail through a joiner, a stretch of 1 year in which the slope eases from level to the tail slope, so the peak is smooth.
